Voice & tone, v1.3Sounds like a teacher who is good company

He gives you the verdict first, reasons it out in the first person, exaggerates for effect, laughs at himself before anyone else, and never makes you feel small. He has marked the paper and knows why the marks were lost, and he would rather show you than lecture you. The humour is the kind a whole classroom laughs at together. Nobody is the target.

Foundations

Five traits, in priority order

TraitIt isIt is not
StraightThe answer first. A clear position.Hedging, "it depends" with no follow-up
SpecificMarks, papers, syllabus codes, real mistakes"Study smart," "manage your time"
FunnySelf-mocking, exaggerated, asides in bracketsJokes at a person, a place or the reader
CurrentKnows how students actually studyA teacher performing as a teenager
On your sideExplains without talking downLecturing, scaring, selling in every paragraph

When two traits pull against each other, this order decides. Straight beats funny: a joke never delays or blurs the answer.

Who is speaking

  • A named teacher, first person, for exam technique, mark schemes, subject guides and reviews the author actually used. A claim like "I have marked this for years" only appears if that teacher actually says so.
  • A named author, for opinion pieces, in first person, labelled "Opinion."
  • "We," SJ Academy's teachers, for system guides, entertainment posts and news. Never the royal "we" of a company talking about its values.
  • One narrator per post. Never switch between "I" and "we." Every post ends with a two-line author note: who wrote or reviewed it, and what they teach.
An AI model never invents a teacher's experience. Where one would help, it leaves a bracket: [teacher to add: a real example from marking].

Humour

General and relatable. Never a named target

The joke is about a situation every student or parent recognises, so everyone can laugh and nobody is the punchline.

The test

A humorous line stays only if it is all four. If it fails one, cut it. A post with no joke is fine. A post with a weak joke is worse than both.

  • True. It exaggerates a real thing.
  • Relatable. Most readers have lived it.
  • General. No school, board, company, city or individual is named or obviously meant.
  • Tasteful. You would say it in class with parents in the room.

What it is about

In rangeOut of range
Exam season, mark scheme wording, study habits everyone hasAny named school, board, company or individual
Classroom moments, family situations everyone sharesThe reader's intelligence, effort or grades
Ourselves: teachers, and tuition centres including this oneAnyone who failed, resat or struggled
Everyday Karachi and Pakistan life every reader sharesReligion, ethnicity, gender, class, area, mental health, money, politics
Works

"The mark scheme calls it evaluation. Students call it the paragraph where you repeat your answer with 'however' in front of it. It scores what you would expect."

Does not work

"Only the exam board could write a question this confusing." It points at an organisation instead of the shared experience: "Whoever wrote question 4 was having a day" makes the same joke about nobody.

Works

"Every family has one relative who is certain A Levels were harder in his day. He is welcome to sit Paper 4."

Does not work

"Failed your AS? Oops." A joke at someone living through a bad result.

Karachi and Pakistan flavour

The readers live here, so the humour should too: load-shedding and the UPS, traffic, relatives who ask about grades, chai, the last-minute crash course, results-day phone calls. Shared situations, never stereotypes about an area, community or type of school. Karachi is the setting, never the target. English only; everyday Pakistani English words (chai, beta, rishta) are fine, full Roman Urdu sentences are not.

load-shedding has a sixth sense for exam season

Placement: never in the short answer, a step, a number, a definition or the call to action. Follow a sarcastic line with a straight one.

Current

Gen Z aware, not Gen Z fluent

This is a teacher who understands the reference and uses it once, correctly. It is not a teacher performing as a teenager.

What awareness means

  • It knows how they study. Past paper sites, 2x speed videos, group chats at 2am, AI tools for explanations.
  • It respects their attention. Answer first. Say how long something takes.
  • Slang: student posts only, two terms per post at most, no quotation marks around it, nothing that needs a search to understand. Slang dates fast, so it is checked before each post goes out.
Works

"If you are reading this the night before Paper 2, you are cooked on content. You are not cooked on technique. That part we can fix by morning."

Does not work

"Stay locked in, king. The grind never stops." Sounds like a caption, not a teacher.

Format

Entertainment posts, the BuzzFeed style

For students only, one for every five guides. Narrator "we." A bold line that is the thing itself, then one to three dry sentences. 7 to 21 items, strongest one last. At least one item where the teachers are the joke.

Mechanics

Sentence craft

  • Short sentences, mostly under 20 words. Paragraphs of one to three sentences.
  • Second person, active voice, plain words. "Examiners award the mark for," not "the mark is awarded for."
  • Numbers as digits for marks, dates, fees and counts.
  • No em dashes. Use a full stop or a comma instead.
  • Two exclamation marks per post, at most. None in system guides, news or posts for parents.
  • No swearing, including the mild kind. One use per post for any strong word (insane, obsessed, brutal).
  • British spelling: enrol, practise (verb), organise, humour.
  • Sentence case for titles and headings.
  • Emoji only where they add value: delete the emoji, and if the line reads the same, leave it out. None in a title, short answer, step, date or call to action.
Before and after

Vague to specific

Before

Time management is very important in the exam, so make sure you plan your time wisely.

After

Paper 1 gives you 75 minutes for 40 marks. That is under two minutes a mark. If your 8-marker takes 25 minutes, you have just paid for it with the 12-marker.

Before

Students must understand that without consistent hard work, good grades cannot be achieved.

After

You are probably working hard enough. You are probably working on the wrong thing.

Before

At SJ Academy, we are passionate about delivering a comprehensive learning experience that empowers students to unlock their potential.

After

We teach to the mark scheme, mark your answers the way an examiner would, and show you where the marks went.

Non-negotiable

The voice is sharp only because the content is right

  • Every factual claim has a source a reader could check: the syllabus, a mark scheme, an examiner report, an official page.
  • Never invent a statistic, a quote, a student story, an examiner comment or a teacher's experience.
  • No guarantees. Never promise a grade. If a fact and a joke conflict, the fact wins and the joke goes.
When we get something wrong: correct the post, say so at the top with the date, plain and first person plural, no spin, no blaming the source. "We got the conversion wrong in the first version of this post. It is fixed now, and we have checked it against the official page."

Ten minutes, before it goes out

  • Every fact has a source or a bracket. Official rules are dated and linked.
  • The short answer fully answers the title. Each section makes a point you could disagree with.
  • No advice that would fit any subject or any exam.
  • Every humorous line is true, relatable, general and tasteful. None in the short answer, steps, numbers or the call to action.
  • Read it as a parent whose child just got a bad result. Nothing stings.
  • Two slang terms at most, none in parent posts. No trend-of-the-month references.
  • No em dashes. Two exclamation marks at most. No swearing. No words from the banned list.
  • One narrator, one reader, one call to action. Author note, updated date and syllabus years present.

Prompt block, for drafting with an AI model

You are drafting a blog post for SJ Academy (sjacademy.pk), a Cambridge O and A Level tuition centre in Karachi.

Voice. Sound like a teacher who is good company: verdict first, first-person reasoning, exaggeration for effect, self-mocking before anything else, never talking down. Five traits, in priority order: straight, specific, funny, current, on the reader's side.

Reader. One reader only: student or parent. Address them as "you." They are in Pakistan, sitting Cambridge exams, reading on a phone.

Narrator. First person as a named teacher, or "we" meaning SJ Academy's teachers. Do not switch. Never invent the narrator's experience, a statistic, a quote or a student story; leave a bracket instead.

Humour. State the level for this post: none, light, regular or full. General and relatable only, never a named target. If a joke needs an object, point at the thing, keep it affectionate, one line only.

Gen Z awareness for student posts only: aware, not fluent. At most two slang terms, used plainly, never stacked.

Style. Short and medium sentences. Active voice, plain words, British spelling, sentence case headings. No em dashes. At most two exclamation marks in the whole post.

Facts. Use only the source material provided and the approved facts register. Mark anything uncertain with a bracket. Date official processes. Never promise a grade.
