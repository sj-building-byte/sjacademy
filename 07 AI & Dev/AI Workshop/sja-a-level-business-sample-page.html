Why marks get lost

Most students do not fail Business because they know nothing.

They lose marks because their writing stays general, ignores the case data, or stops before a clear evaluation. The class is built around fixing that exact problem.

1
Descriptive answers The answer explains a concept but does not connect it to the business in the question.
2
Weak analysis chains The student makes one point, but does not show the effect on costs, revenue, profit, cash flow, or stakeholders.
3
Evaluation without judgement The conclusion repeats both sides instead of giving a supported recommendation.
Reality check

In Business 9609, knowing the topic is only the starting point. Marks come from applying the topic to the case, building cause and effect, and making a reasoned decision.

Paper 1: precise definitions, application, and short analysis.

Students practice how to answer quickly without wasting time on vague paragraphs. The focus is accuracy, business language, and relevant explanation.

ChallengeAnswers stay too general.

Students know terms but do not shape answers to the question.

TrainingMark-focused response patterns.

Class drills short answer structures and case-specific wording.

OutcomeCleaner, faster responses.

Students learn to answer with less filler and more exam value.

Paper 2: data response with stronger application.

Students learn to use the given business data instead of writing memorised textbook points. This is where many AS students can gain marks quickly.

ChallengeCase data is ignored.

The answer could apply to any business, so it does not earn enough application credit.

TrainingEvidence-linked analysis.

Students practice connecting figures, business context, and stakeholder impact.

OutcomeMore relevant answers.

Responses become specific to the business in front of them.

Paper 3: long case analysis and stronger chains.

A2 students need more than topic knowledge. They need to build multi-step business arguments and connect decisions to the case context.

ChallengeAnalysis breaks too early.

Students make a point but do not carry it through to business consequences.

TrainingCause and effect chains.

Class builds structured chains across cost, revenue, profit, risk, and people.

OutcomeMore developed answers.

Students learn how to stretch each point without padding.

Paper 4: recommendation writing and judgement.

Students practice how to compare options, weigh trade-offs, and make a final recommendation that actually answers the question.

ChallengeEvaluation is too balanced.

The final paragraph repeats both sides but does not decide.

TrainingDecision-led evaluation.

Students learn to justify a recommendation using the case and business priorities.

OutcomeSharper judgement.

Answers finish with a clear, defensible business decision.
