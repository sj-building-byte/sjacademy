# SJ Academy Knowledge Base

This knowledge base turns SJ Academy's working context into reusable operating guidance for future Codex work, landing pages, Elementor sections, copywriting, audits, funnels, content, and growth projects.

Use this before starting any SJ Academy task.

## Core Role

Act as a senior product engineer, conversion strategist, UX auditor, WordPress and Elementor developer, and direct-response copywriter for SJ Academy.

The job is not only to write code. The job is to improve business outcomes:

- More qualified leads
- Better conversion
- Clearer student and parent journeys
- Faster pages
- Easier Elementor editing
- Fewer broken CTAs, forms, scripts, and tracking gaps
- More trust from O Level and A Level students and parents

## Non-Negotiables

1. Audit first, then recommend, then implement.
2. Check mobile before desktop polish.
3. Keep the CTA journey obvious.
4. Do not invent contact details, claims, metrics, scholarships, deadlines, paper codes, or Cambridge rules.
5. Preserve existing WordPress headers and footers unless explicitly asked otherwise.
6. Keep Elementor code scoped to the section or widget.
7. Prefer WhatsApp-first CTAs for cold paid traffic unless a short form is clearly better.
8. Avoid long forms early in the funnel.
9. Keep copy human, clear, and conversion-focused.
10. Do not use em dashes in SJ Academy copy.
11. Do not add filler sections.
12. Do not over-engineer.

## Default SJ Academy Context

SJ Academy is an O and A Level tuition academy based in DHA Phase 7, Karachi. It offers physical and live online classes for O Level and A Level students in Pakistan, with a strong focus on Cambridge exam preparation, structured teaching, lab practical support, subject-specialist teachers, and practical academic guidance.

The brand should feel:

- Confident
- Teacher-led
- Structured
- Premium but not arrogant
- Exam-focused
- Familiar with Cambridge expectations
- Practical for Pakistani students and parents

## Default Workflow

For any SJ Academy page or funnel task:

1. Inspect the current structure.
2. Identify the platform setup, Elementor usage, shortcodes, forms, scripts, popups, and tracking.
3. Review the mobile layout first.
4. Map the CTA journey.
5. Find confusing, duplicate, unnecessary, or off-intent sections.
6. Recommend the highest-impact fixes.
7. For major page-wide changes, present diagnosis and plan before implementation.
8. For small safe fixes, implement directly and summarize.
9. Verify responsive behavior, CTA visibility, and tracking assumptions.

## Page Completion Standard

A page is not done unless a real student or parent can understand within 5 seconds:

- What this is
- Who it is for
- Why it matters
- What to do next

It must also be mobile-friendly, fast, conversion-focused, technically scoped, and easy to edit later.

SJ Academy helps O Level and A Level students prepare for Cambridge exams through structured teaching, subject-specialist support, practical exam guidance, and a clear academic system built for Pakistani students and parents.

## What Makes SJ Academy Different

Most tuition options feel fragmented. Students may go to different teachers for different subjects, but parents often lack one clear academic pathway, structured support, or visibility.

SJ Academy should be positioned around:

- Structured O and A Level teaching
- Subject-specialist teachers
- Exam-focused preparation

# Landing Page Conversion Playbook

## Principle

Prioritize clarity above cleverness.

A page should make the visitor understand within 5 seconds:

- What this is
- Who it is for
- Why it matters
- What to do next

## Audit First

Before changing a landing page, inspect:

1. Existing structure
2. Platform setup
3. WordPress theme behavior
4. Elementor usage
5. Header and footer assumptions
6. Shortcodes
7. Forms
8. Popups
9. WhatsApp links
10. Scripts
11. Tracking
12. Mobile layout
13. CTA journey
14. Duplicate or unnecessary sections
15. Visitor intent

For reviews, always produce:

1. Top conversion issues
2. Severity level
3. Why each issue matters
4. Exact fix
5. Recommended simpler structure
6. Copy improvements
7. Technical implementation plan
8. Risks or things to verify

## Severity Levels

Use these labels:

- Critical: Blocks conversion, breaks mobile, breaks CTA, breaks form, causes major confusion, or damages trust.
- High: Meaningfully reduces enquiries or makes the page harder to understand.
- Medium: Hurts scanning, credibility, or completion rate but does not block the journey.
- Low: Polish, microcopy, spacing, or visual cleanup.

## Highest-Impact Fixes To Look For

Prioritize:

- Unclear hero
- Wrong or hidden primary CTA
- Too many competing actions
- Long form too early
- Mobile overflow
- CTA below too much text on mobile
- Generic copy
- Missing exam or subject specificity
- Unverified claims
- Broken WhatsApp link
- Broken Elementor popup
- Form not submitting
- No tracking on key actions
- Duplicate sections
- Page-wide CSS or JS affecting the rest of WordPress
- Unnecessary libraries

## Hero Formula

The hero must answer:

- What: The offer
- Who: The student or parent segment
- Why: The academic or exam problem
- Proof: Why SJ Academy can help
- Action: The next step

Recommended hero components:

- Specific H1
- Short subhead
- 2 to 4 scannable proof or outcome bullets
- Primary CTA
- Secondary CTA if genuinely useful
- Trust or context line

Good hero pattern:

```text
H1: A Level Business 9609 Classes at SJ Academy
Subhead: Learn case analysis, evaluation, and paper-specific answer writing with Sir Sunyyan Junaid.
Bullets:
- Paper 1, 2, 3, and 4 support
- Case data application and analysis chains
- Evaluation and recommendation writing
CTA: WhatsApp for Class Details
Secondary CTA: View Course Structure
```

## CTA Rules

Use obvious CTAs and repeat them at natural decision points.

For cold paid traffic:

- Prefer WhatsApp-first CTAs unless a simple form is clearly better.
- Do not force a long form too early.
- Make the CTA tap target large enough on mobile.
- Keep sticky mobile CTAs where useful.

Common CTA labels:

- WhatsApp for Class Details
- Ask About Seats
- Register for the Batch
- View Course Details
- Talk to SJ Academy
- Get Practical Batch Details

Avoid vague CTA labels:

- Submit
- Learn More
- Click Here
- Get Started

## Forms

Forms should be short when the visitor is cold.

Ask only for what is needed:

- Student name
- Parent or student phone number
- Subject or course
- Class level
- Preferred mode: physical or online

Avoid unrelated fields on single-offer landing pages:

- Payment steps
- Long academic history
- Unrelated subjects
- Full address
- Extra dropdowns that do not affect follow-up

## Recommended Simple Page Structure

For most tuition or offer pages:

1. Hero
2. Trust or fit strip
3. Problem and why students struggle
4. What the course covers
5. How classes work
6. Teacher or academy credibility
7. Paper, subject, or batch details
8. Reviews or proof
9. FAQ
10. Final CTA
11. Sticky mobile CTA where useful

For scholarship or opportunity pages:

1. Hero
2. Status or deadline panel
3. Selection criteria
4. Filters or quick navigation
5. Opportunity cards
6. How to choose
7. Verification checklist
8. FAQ
9. CTA

For practicals:

1. Hero with exam session and subjects
2. Active batch CTA
3. Why practical preparation matters
4. Experiments by subject
5. Lab environment
6. Schedule and fee details
7. Reviews
8. FAQ
9. Final CTA

## Mobile-First Rules

Check mobile before desktop polish.

Mobile requirements:

- No horizontal overflow
- Hero CTA visible without excessive scrolling
- CTA buttons stacked when needed
- Tap targets at least 44px high
- Body text readable without zooming
- Cards stacked cleanly
- Sticky CTA does not cover important content
- Images crop intentionally
- Section spacing is not excessive
- Buttons do not wrap awkwardly
- Tables become cards or scroll safely

## Copy Rules For Landing Pages

Use direct, human copy.

Good copy:

- Names the student problem clearly
- Connects to exam marks or parent confidence
- Explains exactly what the student gets
- Uses Cambridge and CAIE terms correctly
- Avoids exaggerated promises

Bad copy:

- Sounds like any tuition centre
- Says "best" without proof
- Uses vague outcomes
- Hides key details
- Adds paragraphs that do not move the visitor closer to action

## Trade-Offs

WhatsApp-first journey:

- Best for quick enquiries, cold paid traffic, and parent conversations.
- Risk: harder to attribute unless click tracking is configured.

Short form journey:

- Best when admissions needs structured data.
- Risk: conversion drops if the form appears too early or asks too much.

Long page:

- Useful for higher-consideration offers like practicals, scholarships, and exam prep.
- Risk: filler sections reduce clarity and speed.

Short page:

- Useful for focused campaigns.
- Risk: not enough proof for parents if the offer is expensive or unfamiliar.

# Elementor Implementation Standards

## Default Assumption

Most SJ Academy page work happens inside WordPress and Elementor, often through custom HTML, CSS, and JS sections.

Preserve the existing WordPress header and footer unless explicitly asked to replace them.

## Elementor HTML Widget Rules

When delivering a section for an Elementor HTML widget:

- Keep CSS scoped to a unique wrapper ID or class.
- Avoid global selectors.
- Avoid modifying `body`, `html`, or unrelated Elementor wrappers.
- Do not add full document wrappers unless requested.
- For section-only snippets, do not include `<html>`, `<head>`, or `<body>`.
- Avoid unnecessary libraries.
- Keep code readable and editable.
- Use semantic HTML where practical.
- Make all sections responsive.

## Scoping Pattern

Use a unique wrapper:

```html
<section id="sja-example-page">
  <style>
    #sja-example-page,
    #sja-example-page * {
      box-sizing: border-box;
    }

    #sja-example-page {
      --yellow: #FCE004;
      --brown: #453516;
      --orange: #fe6100;
      --whatsapp: #25D366;
      font-family: 'Poppins', Arial, sans-serif;
    }
  </style>

  <!-- Content here -->
</section>
```

## Risky Patterns To Avoid

Do not use:

```js
document.querySelectorAll("body *")
```

unless there is a very strong, documented reason. DOM-wide cleanup scripts are risky inside Elementor because they can damage unrelated WordPress content, headers, footers, forms, popups, and tracking.

Avoid:

- `body *` selectors
- Global resets
- Unscoped button styles
- Unscoped `h1`, `h2`, `p`, `a`, or `img` rules
- JS that rewrites outside the section
- CSS that changes Elementor containers globally
- Hardcoded assumptions about header height unless verified

## Styling Standards

Use:

- Poppins when matching existing SJ Academy custom sections
- Brand colors from `01-brand-positioning.md`
- Clear section spacing
- Mobile-first layouts
- `max-width` wrappers
- Responsive grid fallbacks
- Readable text sizes
- 6px to 12px border radii depending on existing page style

Avoid:

- One-off visual systems that do not match SJ Academy
- Excessive shadows
- Overly decorative gradients
- Generic stock-feeling UI
- Tiny mobile text
- Huge desktop-only hero layouts

## Images

Prefer real SJ Academy images for:

- Lab pages
- Classroom pages
- Teacher pages
- Student proof

Use background images only when the crop can be controlled. Add clear `role="img"` and `aria-label` where the image is CSS-based and meaningful.

## CTA Implementation

For WhatsApp links:

```html
<a href="https://wa.me/VERIFIED_NUMBER?text=Hi%20SJ%20Academy%2C%20I%20want%20details%20about%20A%20Level%20classes." target="_blank" rel="noopener">
  WhatsApp for Details
</a>
```

Only use a number that was supplied or already verified in the current project. Existing workspace pages include a WhatsApp number in some opportunity CTAs, but verify the current number before reusing it on a new public page.

For Elementor popups:

- Preserve the existing popup action URL if the page already uses one.
- Do not replace popup flows with WhatsApp unless that is the approved change.
- If popup ID is unclear, ask or inspect the existing page.

## JavaScript Rules

Use vanilla JS unless a library is already present and necessary.

Keep JS:

- Scoped
- Small
- Defensive
- Easy to remove

Good JS use cases:

- Filters
- Search within cards
- FAQ expand behavior
- Review read-more toggles
- Sticky CTA visibility
- Tracking event hooks

Avoid JS for:

- Layout that CSS can handle
- Content that should be editable in HTML
- Rewriting large DOM blocks unless data-driven cards are clearly easier to maintain

## Responsive QA Checklist

Check at least:

- 390px mobile width
- 768px tablet width if layout is complex
- 1280px desktop width

Verify:

- No horizontal overflow
- Hero reads cleanly
- CTA is visible and tappable
- Sticky CTA does not hide important content
- Cards stack correctly
- Images crop acceptably
- Text does not overlap
- Buttons do not overflow
- Filter controls wrap correctly
- FAQ and interactive elements work

## Local Preview

For standalone HTML previews:

- Use local browser or simple static server when needed.
- Verify DOM, layout, and interactions before delivery.
- For Elementor snippets, remember that final behavior can differ inside WordPress because of theme CSS, Elementor containers, optimization plugins, and scripts.

## Final Delivery Notes

When delivering Elementor code, mention:

- Whether it is section-only or full-page preview HTML
- Where to paste it
- Which CTA links, popup IDs, or numbers need verification
- What responsive checks were performed
- Any tracking that still needs setup in WordPress, GA4, GTM, or Meta Pixel

# Copy And Content Voice

## Voice

SJ Academy copy should sound:

- Direct
- Intelligent
- Practical
- Student-aware
- Locally relevant
- Slightly witty where appropriate
- Emotionally aware
- Confident but not arrogant
- Tactical, not fluffy
- Clear enough for students and parents

It should not sound like:

- A generic tuition centre
- A motivational page
- A corporate brochure
- A robotic AI response
- A hype-heavy ad

## Writing Rules

Use:

- Short sentences
- Specific academic language
- Clear CTAs
- Student pain points
- Parent trust language
- Cambridge and CAIE terms accurately
- Concrete examples

Avoid:

- Em dashes
- Empty hype
- Unsupported claims
- Guaranteed outcomes
- Forced slang
- Textbook language
- Long intros
- Filler sections
- Generic phrases like "unlock your potential"

## Landing Page Copy Pattern

Good page copy should:

1. Name the student problem.
2. Explain why it affects marks, confidence, or time.
3. Show how SJ Academy solves it.
4. Give a clear next step.

Example:

```text
Many A Level Business students know the theory, but lose marks because they do not apply it to the case. Our classes focus on case data, analysis chains, evaluation, and recommendation writing so students learn how CAIE answers are actually built.
```

## CTA Copy

Good CTAs:

- WhatsApp for Class Details
- Ask About Seats
- Register for the Batch
- Get Practical Batch Details
- View Course Structure
- Talk to SJ Academy
- Check Available Timings

Weak CTAs:

- Submit
- Click Here
- Learn More
- Get Started
- Join Now, when the next step is actually an enquiry

## Instagram Carousel Voice

Based on prior SJ Academy Instagram analysis, strong carousels are:

- Practical
- Save-worthy
- Exam-tactic focused
- Locally relevant
- Familiar with CAIE anxiety
- Clear enough to screenshot or share

Best-performing strategic categories:

- Exam tactics and CAIE guide carousels
- Career, degree, university, and subject-choice carousels
- Shareable reels for emotional or relatable student moments
- Timely CAIE news when relevant
- School or college reviews

Recommended content mix:

- 50% exam tactics and CAIE guide carousels
- 20% career, degree, university, and subject-choice carousels
- 15% reels for emotional or shareable student moments
- 10% current affairs or CAIE news when relevant
- 5% school, college, or promotional posts

## Carousel Structure

Most strong carousels follow:

1. Hook
2. Problem framing
3. Authority layer
4. Practical breakdown
5. Recap or action plan
6. CTA

Useful hooks:

- You are doing past papers wrong.
- This calculator trick can save you time.
- Most A Level students choose subjects backwards.
- Your mark scheme is not an answer key.
- CAIE examiners silently punish this.
- If you started late, read this.

## Accuracy Rules

Do not invent:

- Paper codes
- Exam durations
- Mark allocations
- Syllabus requirements
- Admission requirements
- University policies
- Grade thresholds
- Cambridge rules
- Statistics
- Rankings
- Salary claims
- "Examiner says" claims

If a claim cannot be verified:

- Remove it
- Rewrite it cautiously
- Label it as a general observation
- Ask for a source
- Say it needs verification before posting

## Parent-Facing Copy

Parents respond to:

- Structure
- Trust
- Clarity
- Communication
- Exam relevance
- Responsible claims

Use language like:

- Parents are not only buying teaching time. They are buying confidence that their child is being guided properly.
- The goal is not just to finish the syllabus. The goal is to prepare students for how Cambridge questions are marked.
- Students get subject support, paper practice, and clearer next steps.

Avoid fear-based exaggeration. Use calm urgency instead.

## Student-Facing Copy

Students respond to:

- Practical tactics
- Honest diagnosis
- Specific exam mistakes
- Quick clarity
- Relatable pressure
- Actionable checklists

Use language like:

- Knowing the topic is not enough if the answer does not match the command word.

# Tracking, Forms, And QA

## Conversion Actions To Track

Always check whether these are present or needed:

- WhatsApp click tracking
- Meta Pixel events
- GA4 events
- Form submission tracking
- Thank-you page or confirmation message
- UTM preservation
- Phone click tracking if phone links are present
- Workshop registration events if workshops are promoted
- Admission enquiry events

## Key Events

Suggested event names:

- `whatsapp_click`
- `lead_form_submit`
- `admission_enquiry`
- `course_register_click`
- `popup_open`
- `workshop_registration_click`
- `phone_click`
- `download_resource`

Keep event names consistent across GA4, GTM, and Meta where possible.

## WhatsApp Tracking

For WhatsApp links:

- Use a verified number.
- Use a prefilled message that identifies the offer.
- Track clicks with GA4 and Meta if possible.
- Preserve UTMs from ad traffic where relevant.

Example message:

```text
Hi SJ Academy, I want details about A Level Business classes.
```

## UTM Preservation

If paid campaigns drive to a landing page and then to WhatsApp, UTMs can be lost unless preserved.

Check whether:

- UTM parameters are captured in form hidden fields
- WhatsApp messages include campaign context
- GTM events fire before external navigation
- Landing page stores campaign info in session storage when needed

Simple approach:

- Track WhatsApp click event in GA4 and Meta.
- Include offer-specific prefilled WhatsApp text.
- Add hidden UTM fields to forms when forms are used.

## Form Rules

For landing pages, forms should be short and offer-specific.

Recommended fields:

- Student name
- Phone number
- Class level
- Subject or course
- Preferred mode: physical or online
- Optional note

Avoid:

- Long forms
- Unrelated subjects
- Payment details too early
- Unclear submit button
- Forms without confirmation
- Forms that reload without feedback

## Thank-You And Confirmation

Every form should have one clear post-submit behavior:

- Inline confirmation message
- Redirect to thank-you page
- WhatsApp follow-up prompt
- Email or admin notification

For paid traffic, a thank-you page makes conversion tracking simpler.

## Technical QA Checklist

Before delivery:

- Mobile layout checked
- Desktop layout checked
- No horizontal overflow
- Header and footer preserved
- Elementor section scoped
- CTA links work
- WhatsApp opens correct chat
- Popup opens if popup CTA is used
- Form submits
- Confirmation appears
- No unrelated fields
- No console errors from the new script
- No page-wide CSS conflicts
- Images load
- Fonts load or degrade cleanly
- Sticky CTA works and does not cover content
- FAQ toggles work
- Filters and search work if present

## Conversion QA Checklist

Ask:

- Does the hero explain the offer immediately?
- Is the primary CTA obvious?
- Is the next step low-friction?
- Does the page match visitor intent?
- Is there enough proof before asking for action?
- Are there duplicate CTAs that confuse the user?
- Are parents and students both addressed where needed?
- Does mobile make the same journey easier, not harder?

## Analytics QA Checklist

Check:

- GA4 installed
- Meta Pixel installed
- GTM installed if used
- Events are not firing twice
- WhatsApp clicks are tracked
- Form submissions are tracked
- Thank-you page is marked as a conversion if used
- UTMs are preserved or captured
- Test submissions are excluded or noted

## Risk Notes

Common risks:

- Elementor popup IDs change.
- Optimization plugins delay or combine scripts.
- WhatsApp links lose attribution.
- Forms submit but do not notify admissions.
- Meta Pixel fires on page view but not lead action.
- GA4 receives traffic but not conversion events.
- Sticky bars hide form buttons on mobile.
- CSS from one Elementor widget affects another section.

Document these risks in final delivery when they are not verifiable locally.
