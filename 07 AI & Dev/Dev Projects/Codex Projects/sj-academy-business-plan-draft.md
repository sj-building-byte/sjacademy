# SJ Academy business plan draft

Prepared for investor review  
Current draft date: June 10, 2026

## 1. Executive summary

SJ Academy is an O and A Level learning academy based in Karachi, Pakistan. It currently operates from DHA Phase 7 and serves students through O Level tuition, AS Level tuition, A2 Level tuition, A Level Lab Practicals and online classes.

The business is being positioned as a scalable education company, not only a tuition centre. SJ Academy has already proven demand through existing revenue, student trust, academic services, an operating team and a working physical plus online model. The funding is not for testing whether the idea works. It is for expanding a working academy into a stronger physical and digital learning company.

SJ Academy generated PKR 16.13M in revenue from August 2025 to May 2026, its core academic season. Average monthly revenue was PKR 1.61M, and peak monthly revenue reached PKR 2.73M in April 2026. Revenue grew from PKR 577K in August to PKR 2.73M in April, showing strong exam-season demand around O and A Level preparation and practicals.

SJ Academy is raising PKR 13M in exchange for 10% equity. The raise will fund the next growth phase: a new branch in PECHS or a larger central DHA location, improved lab and academic infrastructure, workshops, digital assets, marketing and admissions, operating hires and a stronger repeatable branch model.

After the new location stabilises, management expects annual revenue potential to reach approximately 2.5x the latest academic season, which would imply a target revenue run-rate of about PKR 40.3M compared with PKR 16.13M in the August 2025 to May 2026 season. This is a management target, not a guaranteed forecast, and should be tested against the detailed financial model.

## 2. Business overview

Current services include:

- O Level tuition
- AS Level tuition
- A2 Level tuition
- A Level Lab Practicals
- Online classes

The academy is co-founded by Salman Junaid and Sunyyan Junaid.

Salman Junaid leads academics and teaches Mathematics. He is central to academic quality, teacher direction and student outcomes. He is an NED graduate with more than 12 years of O and A Level Math teaching experience, including teaching experience at KGS and other schools.

Sunyyan Junaid leads growth, brand, marketing, content, operations, business development and expansion strategy. He also teaches A Level Business and brings more than 10 years of growth, content, community and digital marketing experience from the Cloudways and DigitalOcean ecosystem.

SJ Academy is building toward a more structured model: subject-specialist teachers, better parent communication, practical lab support, online classes, future digital products and a repeatable branch system.

## 3. Problem and solution

### The problem

O and A Level students and parents often face a fragmented tuition market. Students may attend different teachers for different subjects, but without one clear academic pathway, progress tracking or coordinated exam strategy.

The main problems include:

- Inconsistent teacher quality across subjects
- Scattered tuition with no structured learning path
- Limited access to A Level lab practical support
- Weak parent visibility into student progress
- Last-minute exam pressure around mocks, practicals and final papers
- Difficulty finding trusted teachers who understand Cambridge expectations

Parents are not only buying teaching time. They are buying confidence that their child is being guided properly toward exam performance.

### The solution

SJ Academy solves this by combining structured O and A Level teaching with practical support, online access and a stronger academic operating model.

The current solution includes:

- Subject-specialist O and A Level teachers
- Physical classes in Karachi
- Online classes for students outside the branch catchment
- A Level Lab Practicals
- Exam-focused teaching built around syllabus coverage and paper practice
- Parent and student communication through academy operations
- A growing academic team across multiple subjects

The next phase strengthens this solution with better lab capacity, mock exam systems, teacher onboarding, student dashboards, online tests, recorded lessons, workbooks, solution guides and a self-serve learning platform.

These numbers show that SJ Academy is operating inside a large, recurring and globally recognised exam pathway. The business does not need to create a new category. It needs to capture more value from an existing market where families already spend on school fees, tuition, practical preparation, exam support, past-paper practice and academic resources.

### Serviceable opportunity for SJ Academy

The total Cambridge market is large, but SJ Academy’s near-term plan should be judged against a more realistic serviceable opportunity. The business is not trying to capture the global Cambridge market immediately. It is expanding through four focused routes:

1. More physical capacity in Karachi through PECHS or a larger central DHA location.
2. O and A Level live online classes for students outside one branch catchment.
3. A Level Lab Practicals, where physical infrastructure creates a stronger competitive position.
4. Workshops and digital academic assets that can grow outside the normal tuition timetable.

SJ Academy’s current average monthly fee is approximately PKR 15,000 per student. Using a 10-month academic season, one student can represent around PKR 150,000 in annual tuition revenue before any additional practicals, workshops or digital products.

The near-term opportunity can be framed through capacity and revenue targets rather than unrealistic market-share claims:

- Price range: PKR 5,000 to PKR 15,000 per attendee depending on workshop type.
- Expected gross margin: 40% to 50%, because workshop operating costs should be lower than full academic delivery.

If the model works, workshops can become a meaningful adjacent revenue line because they use the same venue, brand, content engine and online delivery capability while reducing dependence on the O and A Level academic season.

## 5. Business model

SJ Academy currently earns revenue from academic services tied to O and A Level preparation. The near-term model should stay focused on four revenue pillars: O and A Level tuition, A Level Lab Practicals, workshops and digital academic assets.

### Current revenue lines

| Revenue line | Growth logic |

| A Level practicals expansion | Builds a defensible niche around lab access and exam preparation |
| Workshops | Short-cycle revenue around O and A Level exams, professional skills, freelancing, business and career topics |

### Quarterly expansion targets

These opportunities should be treated as quarterly targets rather than simultaneous priorities. This keeps the plan focused while still showing upside.

| Future line | When to pursue |
|---|---|
| LMS and student dashboard | After core academic systems and student tracking requirements are defined |
| Online test engine | After initial digital assets and question-bank workflows are validated |
| Professional workshops | Business, freelancing, communication, AI tools, marketing and workplace skills |
| Corporate trainings | B2B training for companies, teams, founders and professional groups |
| IELTS mock tests | Adjacent exam-prep opportunity |
| Entrance exam preparation | SAT, MCAT, IBA, IoBM and other admissions tests |
| Solution eBooks and workbooks | Digital and print learning products |
| Career counselling and summer camps | Adjacent services for students and parents |
| Stationery and merchandise | Smaller but relevant academy-linked sales |

The current teacher payout model is based on a percentage of students, generally ranging from 50% to 70%. SJ Academy plans to move toward a fixed fee per batch model. This matters because a fixed batch model can improve margin visibility, make pricing easier to manage and reduce dependence on variable teacher splits as the academy scales.

## 8. Investor offer, risks, and closing case

### Investor offer

SJ Academy is raising PKR 13M in exchange for 10% equity.

This implies a PKR 130M post-money valuation. The valuation should be understood as a growth-stage valuation for a business moving from one proven branch into a multi-branch and digital education company. It should not be judged only as a valuation of the current branch.

Investors can expect potential returns through:

- A 10% share of distributable annual profits, aligned with the equity stake
- Dividend distributions every two months after one year of operations with the new branch, subject to cash position and agreed reinvestment needs
- Valuation growth from branch expansion and digital products
- A possible future exit or buyback structure
- Final exit, buyback and shareholder terms to be agreed in investor documentation

### Key investor return logic

The investment case depends on four levers:

1. More physical capacity through a new branch or larger location.
2. Better margins over time by moving teacher compensation toward fixed fees per batch.
3. Wider reach through online classes.
4. Workshops and digital assets that are less limited by classroom seats and teacher hours.

If SJ Academy increases capacity from 35 to 45 students at one point to 80 to 100 students at one point, it can serve more students during peak demand periods. With an average monthly fee of around PKR 15,000, the expansion gives the business room to grow revenue if admissions, teacher quality and branch execution are managed properly.

Management expects the new location and related growth initiatives to create potential for approximately 2.5x the latest academic season revenue after stabilisation. This target should be supported by the financial sheet, including assumptions for student count, pricing, teacher payouts, rent, staff costs, marketing costs, workshop contribution and digital asset revenue.

### Main risks and mitigation

| Risk | Why it matters | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|
| Teacher quality dependence | Education businesses can weaken if teacher standards vary | Stronger teacher onboarding, academic direction and batch-level systems |
| Seasonality | Revenue is stronger around exam preparation periods | Use August to May reporting, build workshops, online products and off-season offerings |
| Branch execution risk | New locations can increase fixed costs before revenue matures | Launch by academic season, choose PECHS or central DHA carefully, track capacity and admissions weekly |
| Marketing cost risk | Paid acquisition can become expensive | Balance ads with referrals, school outreach, seminars, content, SEO and parent trust |
| Conversion tracking gap | Current analytics screenshots show traffic growth but key events are not yet tracking admissions actions | Configure WhatsApp clicks, form submissions, calls, workshop registrations and admission enquiries as conversion events |
| Operational inconsistency | Scaling can weaken student experience | Hire branch management, admissions, admin and academic coordination support |
| Workshop execution risk | Weekly attendance targets may take time to build | Start with pilot topics, track attendance and gross margin, then repeat proven formats |
| Digital product adoption risk | Digital assets and platform features may take time to monetize | Start with practical exam resources, tests, workbooks and recorded lessons tied to proven student needs |
| Founder dependency | Growth may depend too heavily on Salman and Sunyyan | Build teacher systems, operating roles, documented processes and repeatable branch playbooks |

### Closing case

SJ Academy has a strong starting point: existing revenue, more than 200 peak-season students on a rolling basis, a recognised academic niche, a team of 12 teachers, physical and online delivery, and a clear expansion plan.

The next stage is about scale. The investment unlocks more capacity, better academic infrastructure, digital products, stronger admissions and the operating team needed to turn one successful academy into a repeatable education business.

The strongest version of the opportunity is not simply another tuition centre. It is a multi-branch and digital O and A Level learning company built around a recurring Cambridge exam market, trusted teachers, practical exam support and scalable academic products.
