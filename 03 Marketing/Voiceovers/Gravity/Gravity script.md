# Gravity: script to record

Read it like you're telling a friend something cool, not like a lesson. Smile while you talk; it shows in the voice. Take a breath between paragraphs. If you trip over a line, pause and say the whole line again. I'll cut the mistakes.

---

Drop a pen.
It falls.
Every. Single. Time.

But why down?
Why not sideways? Or up?

Here's the secret.
Everything with mass pulls on everything else.
You pull on your phone, and your phone pulls on you.
It's just a very, very tiny pull.

But Earth is huge.
About six million billion billion kilograms.
So Earth's pull wins.
It tugs everything towards its centre.
That's gravity.

Now the weird part.
Drop a heavy ball and a light ball together.
They land together.
Gravity speeds everything up by the same amount.
About 9.8 metres per second, every second.

So why does a feather float down slowly?
Air gets in the way.
In 1971, an astronaut dropped a hammer and a feather on the Moon, where there's no air.
They hit the ground at the same time.

The Moon is smaller, so its gravity is weaker.
You'd weigh about one sixth of what you weigh here.
Your mass doesn't change.
Only your weight does.

So next time you drop something,
that's Earth, pulling it in.

---

## How to record (5 minutes)
1. **Room:** the quietest one you have, with soft things around (curtains, a sofa, a bed). Turn off the fans and the AC for the take.
2. **Phone:** use the Voice Memos app on your iPhone. Hold it about a hand's width (15 cm) from your mouth, slightly to the side so your breath doesn't hit it.
3. **Read it twice,** in two separate recordings. Do the second one a little more energetic than feels natural, because energy always sounds lower on playback.
4. **Before you start,** stay silent for 2 seconds so I can measure the room noise.
5. **Send both takes:** Save them in this folder (`03 Marketing/Voiceovers/Gravity/`). Any name is fine.

You don't need to time anything. I'll fit the visuals to how you actually say it.
