# Profit isn't cash (talking-head script, about 75 seconds)

Talk to the camera like you're telling a student a story. Short pauses between sections help me cut in graphics. If you stumble, pause and repeat the whole line.

---

This shop made a profit.
And it still went bust.
How?

Meet Ali. He opens a small shop selling school supplies.

In his first month, a big school orders five lakh rupees of stock. Amazing, right?
It cost Ali three lakh to buy that stock.
Rent: eighty thousand. Salaries: seventy thousand.

So on paper, Ali made a profit of fifty thousand rupees.

But here's the catch.
The school says, "We'll pay you in ninety days."

Ali still has to pay his supplier now. And his rent. And his staff.
He started the month with four lakh in the bank.
Now he's fifty thousand short.

He can't pay the rent.
Profitable on paper. Broke in real life.

That's the difference.
Profit is what you've earned.
Cash is what's actually in the bank, right now.

And when bills are due, only cash counts.

So how do businesses fix it?
Chase customers to pay faster.
Ask suppliers for more time to pay.
Use an overdraft for short gaps.
Or sell for cash, not on credit.

Remember this for your exam:
A business can be profitable and still fail, because it runs out of cash.

Profit on paper. Cash pays the bills.

---

## The numbers (checked)
- Revenue Rs 500,000, sold on 90 days' credit, so no cash comes in this month.
- Costs: stock Rs 300,000, rent Rs 80,000, salaries Rs 70,000, a total of Rs 450,000.
- Profit = 500,000 − 450,000 = **Rs 50,000**.
- Cash: starts at Rs 400,000. Nothing comes in and Rs 450,000 goes out, so it ends at **−Rs 50,000**.
- Accounting point (accruals): revenue counts when the sale is made, even if the customer pays later. That's why profit and cash can differ.
- The fixes are standard syllabus methods for improving cash flow: collecting from customers (trade receivables) faster, longer credit from suppliers (trade payables), overdrafts, and cash sales.

## How to shoot it (iPhone)
1. **Vertical**, back camera, 4K at 30 fps. Phone at eye level, about an arm's length away, on a stand or a pile of books.
2. **Framing:** your head and shoulders, eyes about a third of the way down the frame. Leave some space above your head; I'll crop and zoom.
3. **Light:** face a window, so daylight falls on your face. Don't sit with the window behind you.
4. **Background:** plain and tidy, not too close behind you. A bookshelf or wall is fine.
5. **Sound:** quiet room, fan and AC off. If you have wired earphones with a mic or a clip-on mic, use it. If not, record a backup on a second phone in Voice Memos, just out of frame near your mouth, and clap once at the start so I can sync the two.
6. **Clothes:** a plain solid colour. Avoid thin stripes and white.
7. **Delivery:** look into the lens, not at the screen. Smile on "Meet Ali". Keep your energy a little higher than feels natural.
8. **Takes:** do the whole script in one take or in sections; either works. Leave 2 seconds of silence at the start and end.
