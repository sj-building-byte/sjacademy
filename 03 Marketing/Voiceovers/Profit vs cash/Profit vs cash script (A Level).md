# Profit isn't cash: A Level version (talking-head script, about 90 seconds)

For A Level Business (9609, finance and accounting). Talk to the camera like you're telling a student a story. Short pauses between sections help me cut in graphics. If you stumble, pause and repeat the whole line.

---

This business made a profit.
And it still went bust.
How?

Meet Ali. He runs a stationery supplier.

A big school orders five lakh rupees of stock. On credit. They'll pay in ninety days.
The stock cost Ali three lakh. Rent and salaries: another one and a half lakh.

So his income statement shows a profit of fifty thousand rupees.

But profit counts the sale when it's made, not when the money arrives.
That five lakh isn't cash. It's a trade receivable. Money he's owed.

Meanwhile, Ali has to pay his supplier, his rent and his staff. Now.
He started with four lakh in the bank. He ends the month fifty thousand short.

Profitable. But illiquid.
And a business that can't pay its bills on time can be forced to close, even while it's making a profit.

This is a working capital problem.
And if Ali takes on even more big orders without the cash to fund them, that's called overtrading.

So how does he fix it?
Chase the school to pay sooner.
Ask suppliers for longer credit terms.
Arrange an overdraft for the gap.
Or sell some customers' debts to a factoring company for quick cash.

But here's the A Level bit. Every fix has a catch.
Push customers too hard, and they may go elsewhere.
Delay suppliers, and you could lose discounts or goodwill.
Overdrafts charge interest.
And factoring means giving up part of what you're owed.

So the best answer depends on the business.

For your exam:
Profit is not the same as cash.
A profitable business can still fail if it runs out of liquidity.

Profit on paper. Cash pays the bills.

---

## The numbers (checked)
- Revenue Rs 500,000, sold on 90 days' credit, so no cash comes in this month. It sits in trade receivables.
- Costs: stock Rs 300,000, rent and salaries Rs 150,000 (Rs 80,000 + Rs 70,000), a total of Rs 450,000.
- Profit = 500,000 − 450,000 = **Rs 50,000** (accruals: revenue is recognised when the sale is made).
- Cash: starts at Rs 400,000. Nothing comes in and Rs 450,000 goes out, so it ends at **−Rs 50,000**.

## Syllabus terms used
- Trade receivables, trade payables, working capital, liquidity, overtrading, overdraft, debt factoring.
- Evaluation: the trade-off for each fix (customer goodwill, supplier discounts, interest cost, factoring fee or discount).
