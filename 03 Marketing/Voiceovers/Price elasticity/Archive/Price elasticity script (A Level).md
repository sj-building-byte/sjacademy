# Price cut = more money? (A Level Business 9609, talking-head script, about 100 seconds)

Why this topic: Cambridge's June 2024 examiner report says "elasticity is a challenging concept", that candidates misunderstood "the negative signs for both PED and YED", and that "weaker answers simply assumed that an increase in revenue would mean an increase in profits". This video fixes all three.

Talk to the camera like you're settling an argument with a student. Pause between sections. If you stumble, pause and repeat the line.

---

Your favourite burger place cuts its prices by twenty percent.
Do they make more money?

Most of you just said yes.
And that's exactly how you lose marks.

Let's fix it.

Price elasticity of demand tells you how much people react when the price changes.
Percentage change in quantity demanded, divided by percentage change in price.

So. Burgers go from one thousand rupees to eight hundred. That's minus twenty percent.
Sales jump from a hundred a day to a hundred and forty. Plus forty percent.

Forty divided by minus twenty. PED is minus two.

Keep the minus sign. Price and demand move in opposite directions, and examiners check that you know it.

Minus two means elastic, because it's bigger than one if you ignore the sign. People reacted a lot.

Now look at revenue.
Before: a hundred burgers times a thousand. One lakh.
After: a hundred and forty times eight hundred. One lakh twelve thousand.

Revenue went up. So profit went up, right?

Not so fast.

Each burger costs five hundred rupees to make.
Before, each burger made five hundred. Times a hundred: fifty thousand.
After, each one makes just three hundred. Times a hundred and forty: forty-two thousand.

Revenue up. Profit down.

That's the trap. Elasticity tells you about revenue. Costs tell you about profit.

And it works the other way too. Petrol goes up ten percent, and people only buy two percent less, because they still need to get to work. PED is minus zero point two. That's inelastic. Raise the price, and revenue rises.

Now, the A Level bit. Before you recommend a price cut, ask:
Can the kitchen even make forty percent more burgers?
Will the place next door cut its prices too?
And is that PED estimate even reliable, or is it based on last year's data?

For your exam:
Elastic demand plus a price cut means more revenue.
But never say more profit until you've checked the costs.

Revenue is not profit. Don't let a price cut fool you.

## The numbers (checked)
- PED = %ΔQd ÷ %ΔP = +40% ÷ −20% = **−2** (elastic, |PED| > 1).
- Revenue: 100 × Rs 1,000 = Rs 100,000, then 140 × Rs 800 = Rs 112,000 (up 12%).
- With a variable cost of Rs 500 per burger: 100 × (1,000 − 500) = Rs 50,000, then 140 × (800 − 500) = Rs 42,000. Fixed costs don't change with the price cut, so profit falls by Rs 8,000 whatever they are.
- Petrol: +10% price, −2% quantity, so PED = −0.2 (inelastic). A price rise increases revenue.
- Evaluation points used: production capacity, competitor reaction, how reliable the PED estimate is.

## Syllabus and exam points covered
- PED formula and the negative sign; elastic vs inelastic (|PED| above or below 1).
- Revenue vs profit after a price change (the examiner's main criticism).
- Evaluation in context, not a summary.
