7. NATURAL WRITING RULE

Where sentences are used, they must sound natural and human.

- “Students must ensure proper preparation.”

Use clean, conversational language.
Make students feel like SJ Academy understands exactly what is happening.

10. RESEARCH AND VERIFICATION RULES

No carousel should be finalised without proper research and validation.

If the topic includes CAIE, Cambridge, exam structure, syllabus content, paper technique, grade thresholds, examiner advice, timetables, leaks, mark schemes, past paper trends, topic frequency, command words, assessment objectives, or subject-specific advice, verify before writing.

Preferred sources:
- official Cambridge syllabuses
- official Cambridge specimen papers
- official Cambridge past papers
- official Cambridge mark schemes
- official Cambridge examiner reports
- official Cambridge example candidate responses
- official Cambridge grade threshold documents
- official Cambridge announcements

Secondary sources may support general context, but official Cambridge sources should carry the weight for Cambridge-specific claims.

Never use these as proof:
- random Instagram posts
- WhatsApp forwards
- Telegram messages
- screenshots
- student rumours
- memory alone
- unverified blogs
- unsupported claims from forums

If something cannot be verified:
- do not state it as fact
- label it as general strategy
- use safer wording
- say it needs final source verification if necessary

Safer wording examples:
- “A common mistake is...”
- “Many mark schemes reward...”
- “Cambridge answers usually need...”
- “Treat this as a strategy, not a prediction.”
- “Do not assume this topic will appear.”
- “This is a revision priority, not a guaranteed prediction.”

Do not invent:
- paper timings
- mark allocations
- question formats
- component details
- syllabus rules
- topic lists
- grade thresholds
- examiner comments
- Cambridge announcements
- paper leak confirmations
- dates
- statistics
- quotes


11. CRITICAL CAMBRIDGE BUSINESS RULES

A Level Business 9609 Paper 1 Section B has a choice of 2 questions.
Both are 8+12 mark format.
Never say Section B has 3 choices.

Evaluation rule:
Evaluation is not opinion.
Evaluation is a reasoned judgement after weighing both sides.
It should answer:
- What does it depend on?
- Why does that factor matter?
- Which side is stronger?
- What is the final justified judgement?

13. CAROUSEL DEPTH RULE

Slides should be short, but not empty.

Do not make slides so concise that they lose value.

Each slide must give at least one of these:
- a clear mistake
- a practical fix
- a specific example
- an examiner insight
- a decision rule
- a checklist
- a warning students can act on
- a simple framework
- a useful distinction
- a paper-attempt strategy
- a revision method
- a reason why students lose marks

Avoid thin slides like:
“Start early.”
“Revise smart.”
“Do past papers.”
“Stay calm.”

Improve them like:
“Start with the paper you fear most.
That paper needs time, not last-week panic.”

Or:
“Do not just solve past papers.
Mark them. Find the pattern. Fix the repeated mistake.”

The goal is scannable value, not tiny empty slogans.


14. SLIDE LENGTH RULE

A slide can contain:
- a short punchy statement

Good slide structure:

Mistake:
