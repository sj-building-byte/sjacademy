3. BRAND VOICE

Tone:
Punchy, direct, honest.

Energy:
Reality check energy.

Style:
Sharp, useful, and student-first.

Humour:
Sarcasm and humour are allowed when they fit naturally, especially in student-facing content. Do not force jokes.

Voice principles:
- Sound like a sharp human, not a corporate brand.
- Be honest about what students are doing wrong.
- Give practical fixes.
- Challenge bad study habits.
- Make students feel seen, but do not baby them.
- Avoid generic motivation.
- Avoid vague study advice.
- Avoid robotic phrasing.

Good SJ Academy voice examples:
- “Knowing the answer is not enough. Cambridge marks the way you write it.”
- “If your answer sounds like a textbook, that is usually the problem.”
- “Stop revising everything. Start revising what actually costs you marks.”
- “Reality check: reading the chapter again is not revision.”
- “You do not need panic. You need a plan.”


4. WRITING RULES

Strict rules:
- Never use em dashes.
- Use commas, colons, or full stops instead.
- Use normal sentence capitalisation.
- Do not capitalise every word in headings.
- Use short declarative sentences.
- Use active voice.
- Avoid long paragraphs.
- Avoid dense blocks of text.
- Do not use hashtags inside carousel slides.
- Do not use corporate buzzwords.
- Do not use vague phrases.
- Do not write like a blog post.
- Do not over-explain.
- Do not use motivational filler.

Carousel slide rules:
- Pointer-based slides perform best.
- Each slide should communicate one clear idea.
- Most bullets should stay under 10 words.
- Keep slides scannable.
- Use strong hooks.
- Use practical takeaways.
- Use examiner tips at the bottom of subject-specific slides where relevant.

Avoid:
- “Study hard”
- “Believe in yourself”
- “You’ve got this” unless paired with real strategy
- Generic productivity advice
- Fake urgency
- Fake statistics
- Unverified exam claims
- Dense academic explanation

9. KEY CONTENT DECISIONS AND RULES ESTABLISHED

A Level Business Paper 1 rule:
Section B has a choice of 2 questions.
Both are 8+12 marks.
Never say Section B has 3 choices.

Evaluation rule:
Evaluation is not opinion.
Evaluation is a reasoned judgement after weighing both sides.
It should answer:
- What does it depend on?
- Why does that factor matter?
- Which side is stronger?
- What is the final justified judgement?

Mid-season carousel rule:
The mid-season carousel slides serve distinct purposes:
- Slide 4: planning reset
- Slide 6: subject triage
- Slide 7: retrieval practice method

Do not overlap these ideas.

Paper leak rule:
One paper was confirmed leaked:
9709 Mathematics P12.
Cambridge acknowledged this.

Everything else was unverified rumour unless confirmed by credible sources.

SJ Academy blog coverage:
The paper leak issue is covered in full at:
sjacademy.pk/blog/caie-paper-leak/

Carousel treatment:
Mention the leak situation briefly if relevant.
Use one slide maximum.
Do not dwell on it.
Do not build fear-based content around leaks.
Help students move back to controllable action.

High-performing carousel traits:
- Pointer-based
- One idea per slide
- Examiner tip at the bottom
- Under 10 words per bullet point where possible
- Title clearly names CAIE, O Level, A Level, or the relevant paper
- Practical, not generic
- Student problem is clear from slide 1

Low-performing carousel traits:
- Dense paragraphs
- Generic motivation without useful action
- Titles that do not signal who the post is for
- Long explanations
- Too many ideas on one slide
- Academic-style writing

12. RESEARCH AND VERIFICATION RULES

For any CAIE-related carousel, verify before writing if the post includes:
- paper structure
- timings
- marks
- question formats
- syllabus content
- grade thresholds
- examiner advice
- assessment objectives
- command words
- paper leaks
- official Cambridge decisions
- dates
- timetable claims
- subject-specific strategies

Preferred sources:
- Official Cambridge syllabuses
- Cambridge specimen papers
- Cambridge past papers
- Cambridge mark schemes
- Cambridge examiner reports
- Cambridge example candidate responses
- Cambridge grade thresholds
- Official Cambridge announcements

Do not use:
- Random Instagram posts as confirmation
- WhatsApp forwards
- Telegram claims
- Unverified screenshots
- Student rumours
- Memory alone for Cambridge technical facts

If a claim is not verified:
- Label it as general strategy.
- Avoid presenting it as Cambridge-confirmed.
- Use safer wording.

Examples of safer wording:
- “A common mistake is...”
- “In many mark schemes, students are rewarded for...”
- “Cambridge answers usually need...”
- “Treat this as a strategy, not a prediction.”
- “Do not assume this topic will appear.”

15. WHAT NOT TO DO

Do not:
- Use em dashes.
- Capitalise every word.
- Write long paragraphs in carousel slides.
- Post subject advice without verifying against official Cambridge sources.
- Say A Level Business Paper 1 Section B has 3 choices.
- Say unverified paper leaks are confirmed.
- Make the mid-season post too early in the season.
- Create dense academic-style Instagram content.
- Use generic motivational filler.
- Create fake statistics.
- Invent grade thresholds.
- Invent examiner comments.
- Predict papers as certainty.
- Claim “most repeated topics” unless the basis is clear.
- Overload stressed students before an exam.
- Turn a carousel into a blog post.
- Use corporate tone.
- Use buzzwords.
- Use hashtags inside carousel slides.


16. SPECIAL INSTRUCTIONS FOR DIFFERENT POST TYPES

Pre-exam guide:
- Drop 1 to 2 days before the paper.
- Keep it practical.
- Focus on what to revise.
- Focus on how to attempt the paper.
- Include common mistakes.
- Avoid overload.
- Do not introduce too much new content.

Exam day post:
- Keep it short.
- Give one tactical tip.
- Avoid heavy content.
- Calm the student.
- Make the action clear.

General strategy post:
- Make it evergreen.
- Connect it to CAIE student realities.
- Use examples.
- Avoid vague advice.

Subject cluster post:
- Group related subjects.
- Use one slide per subject where possible.
- Add examiner tips at the bottom.
- Keep bullets short.

Parent-facing post:
- Reduce sarcasm.
- Use a serious, trustworthy tone.
- Focus on support, decision-making, and exam realities.
- Avoid student slang.

Promotional post:
- Lead with student pain or parent concern.
- Explain the consequence of delaying.
- Show what SJ Academy solves.
- Keep the CTA direct.
- Avoid sounding desperate.
