## Accuracy check (00 Start Here/ACCURACY.md)
- Definition: PED = % change in quantity demanded ÷ % change in price. Correct for 9609.
- Worked numbers are examples, not data: burgers price +10%, quantity −30% → PED = −3 (|PED| > 1, elastic). Petrol price +10%, quantity −2% → PED = −0.2 (|PED| < 1, inelastic). Both solved twice. The negative sign is kept, and the "ignoring the minus sign" note covers the common exam slip.
- Diagrams honest: both charts use identical axes, and the same +10% price rise is 100 px on each. Burgers' quantity falls 240 px, petrol's 30 px, so the burger quantity drop draws 8× the petrol one (the % ratio is 15×; the charts have no numeric axes and the % labels carry the numbers). Plain labels ("old price", "new price", "old", "new"), no subscripts.
- Claims: petrol demand is inelastic (few substitutes, a need) and one burger shop's demand is elastic (close substitutes nearby). Both are standard 9609 points. No petrol prices or figures stated.
