# Explainer: Supply and demand, explained by mangoes (no voiceover)

- workflow: general-video (text-led explainer, no voice)
- v2 rebuilt after Sunyyan's review. v1 (in archive/) mixed up moving along a curve with the curve shifting, never showed why the price falls, ended on an abstract arrow table with misaligned columns, and the graph was off-centre.
- What v2 teaches:
  1. Demand: a dot moves along the line ("expensive → buy a few", "cheap → buy lots")
  2. Supply: the same for sellers
  3. The price settles where the two match
  4. Mango season: the same supply line slides right, with "before June" and "June supply" labelled
  5. The mechanism: at the old price there are extra mangoes nobody buys; the price falls until the gap closes
  6. The big idea: price changes move you along the curve; anything else (season, weather, income) shifts the whole curve. More supply means a lower price, less supply a higher one.
- Layout: the graph is centred (axes x 180 to 900, curves symmetric about x 540). Every point is computed from the line equations: E1 (540, 870), E2 (615, 939.8), surplus at the old price from x 540 to 690.

## Accuracy check (00 Start Here/ACCURACY.md)
- Standard axes. Law of demand and law of supply shown as movements along the curves.
- An increase in supply is a rightward shift: more offered at every price.
- At the old price there is a surplus, so the price falls until quantity demanded equals quantity supplied. The new price is lower and the quantity higher. All computed.
- Shift factors are correct: season and weather shift supply; income shifts demand.
- No unverified prices. The season claim is general (peaks in June and July).
