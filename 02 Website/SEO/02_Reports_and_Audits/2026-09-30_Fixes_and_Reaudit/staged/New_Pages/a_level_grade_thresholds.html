What Is an A Level Grade Threshold?

A grade threshold is the minimum raw mark needed for each grade. Cambridge sets thresholds after marking, based on how hard that session's papers turned out to be. A harder paper gets a lower threshold, so the same grade represents the same standard across sessions.

Why do thresholds change every session?

Papers differ slightly in difficulty from one session to the next. Adjusting the threshold keeps grades fair. That is why an A in one year may need a different raw mark from an A in another year.

How is A* decided at A Level?

A* is awarded only on the full A Level. Cambridge's threshold tables list the A* mark for each A Level option alongside the other grades. It is not available on AS Level on its own.

Are variants 1, 2 and 3 graded differently?

Yes. Papers for different time zones are not identical, so each variant has its own component thresholds. Each option code, which is a specific set of components, also has its own overall threshold. Use the row that matches the option code on your statement of entry.

How to Use Thresholds in Your Revision

  • Mark your timed past papers against the mark scheme, then compare your total with the threshold for that same session.
  • Aim a safe margin above the A or A* line, since thresholds move between sessions.
  • Track which paper loses you the most marks and focus practice there.
