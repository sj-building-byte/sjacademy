Updated October 2026. Covers the 2026, 2027 and 2028 syllabus.

Summary. A Level Business (9609) has four written papers and no multiple choice. This guide shows where the marks sit on each paper (on a 12-marker, 6 go to evaluation and only 2 to analysis), what each command word wants, how to write a judgement, and how to spend your last week.

You have the notes. You have the definitions. You marked your last past paper yourself, generously 😅. Then the real mark scheme arrived and your 12-marker got 5.

That gap is rarely knowledge. It is where the marks sit, and nobody shows you the layout. This guide does, paper by paper.

How is the 9609 exam set up?

Four papers across two years. Papers 1 and 2 make the AS Level. Papers 3 and 4 add the A2 content.

PaperTime and marksLevelWhat you get
1: Business Concepts 11 hour 15 minutes, 40 marksASSection A: four short questions. Section B: one essay from a choice of two
2: Business Concepts 21 hour 30 minutes, 60 marksASTwo data response questions, six parts each
3: Business Decision-Making1 hour 45 minutes, 60 marksA2Five questions on a case study
4: Business Strategy1 hour 15 minutes, 40 marksA2Two essays on a case study

Source: Cambridge International AS & A Level Business 9609 syllabus for 2026, 2027 and 2028.

Every paper is written. If your plan involved guessing C, it needs a new plan.

Paper 2 allows 1.5 minutes a mark. Paper 3 allows about 1.75. Papers 1 and 4 allow just under 2. That includes reading and planning, so your writing has to be quicker than it feels.

Where do the marks sit?

Every question is marked on four skills: knowledge (AO1), application (AO2), analysis (AO3) and evaluation (AO4). The split changes with the question.

QuestionMarksSplitWhere it appears
Define22 knowledgePaper 1, Section A part (a)
Explain31 knowledge, 2 applicationPaper 1, Section A part (b). Paper 2, parts (a)(ii) and (b)(ii)
Analyse one impact51 knowledge, 2 application, 2 analysisPaper 1, Question 4
Analyse82 knowledge, 2 application, 4 analysisPaper 1 essay part (a). Paper 2 part (c). Paper 3, Questions 1 and 2
Evaluate122 knowledge, 2 application, 2 analysis, 6 evaluationPaper 1 essay part (b). Paper 2 part (d). Paper 3, the 12-mark part of Questions 3 and 4, and Question 5
Strategy essay203 knowledge, 2 application, 8 analysis, 7 evaluationPaper 4, Questions 1 and 2
remember

On a 12-marker, analysis is worth 2 marks and evaluation is worth 6.

Read the 12-mark row twice. Cambridge's examiners say the same thing in their reports: answers are heavy on knowledge and analysis, and light on application and evaluation (principal examiner report, 9609/11, June 2024).

The harder the paper, the more evaluation is worth: 15% of Paper 1, 20% of Paper 2, 30% of Paper 3 and 35% of Paper 4 (syllabus).

Most students spend most of the answer on the 2 marks they were already going to get. It is a loyal strategy. It is not a good one.

What does each command word want?

Cambridge publishes the list for this syllabus. Here is what each one asks of your answer.

Command wordCambridge saysWhat to do
IdentifyName, select or recogniseWrite one or two words. A paragraph earns the same mark and costs you time
DefineGive precise meaningGive two clear parts. "Costs that do not change" is half a definition. Add "when output changes"
CalculateWork out from given facts, figures or informationWrite the formula, then the working, then the answer with its unit. A wrong answer with clear working still earns marks
ExplainSet out reasons, make the relationships clear, say why and how, with evidenceGive one reason, develop it, and tie it to the business
AnalyseExamine in detail to show meaning, identify elements and the relationship between themBuild a chain: because, therefore, as a result
Assess, EvaluateMake an informed judgement. Judge the quality, importance, amount or valueWeigh both sides, then decide, and put the decision in the business's words
Advise, JustifySuggest a course of action. Support a case with evidencePick one option, back it with evidence from the case, and say what could go wrong

Phrases such as "To what extent" also appear. Treat them as evaluation questions.

"Discuss" feels like permission to write everything you know. It is not on Cambridge's list for this syllabus.

Why does "this lowers costs" score one mark?

Because it is one link. Examiners reward three-stage reasoning, built with words such as because, therefore, as a result and this leads to (principal examiner report, 9609/13, June 2024).

Sample, one link: Training improves productivity.

Sample, a chain: Training teaches the bakery's decorators to finish a cake faster, so each decorator completes more orders per shift. That cuts the labour cost of each cake, which lifts the profit on every cake sold. (The bakery is in there on purpose.)

three links, not one

One link is a claim. Two links is a hunch. Three is analysis.

Aim for the third link every time. In an 8-marker, two fully developed points beat four thin ones.

What counts as using the case?

Naming the business does not count. The examiner report gives the example: for a car manufacturer, writing "car manufacturer" earns no application, but engines, tyres and car models do. For an internet retailer, think shopping cart, website, delivery and warehouse (9609/13, June 2024).

On Papers 2, 3 and 4 the case also gives you numbers. Quote them.

Sample, no context: Profit was low.

Sample, in context: Profit fell from $0.05 million to a loss of $0.1 million.

Before you write, jot five words from the business. For a hotel: rooms, guests, bookings, housekeeping, peak season. Use at least two of them in your final paragraph.

Repeating the business's name five times is not application. It is a polite way of saying nothing.

How do you write the 6 evaluation marks?

Start before you write. Underline four things in the question: the concept, the claim word, the outcome and the context.

Here is a real 12-marker, from 9609/12, May/June 2026, question 6(b):

"The only way to stop industrial action in a public sector railway business is to remove trade union involvement in the workplace." Evaluate this view. [12]
PartIn this question
ConceptTrade unions and industrial action
Claim word"Only way". One alternative breaks it: negotiation, better pay, employee participation
OutcomeStopping industrial action. Not profit
ContextA public sector railway: drivers, signal staff, commuters, safety

The claim word tells you the shape of the essay.

  • "Most important": weigh the concept against two rivals, then pick one.
  • "Essential", "only way", "all": find one case where the statement is false.
  • "Evaluate the importance of": bring a rival yourself and say what it depends on.

Then finish with this frame: For [business], [concept] is / is not [the claim] because [reason from the context]. It depends on [condition]. The deciding factor is [x].

Most conclusions say both sides have merit, it depends on many factors, and the business should weigh its options carefully before deciding. Nothing was decided.

Sample, a summary: Trade unions have advantages and disadvantages, so it depends on the situation.

Sample, a judgement: Removing union involvement is not the only way to stop strikes on a state railway. Drivers and signal staff strike over pay and safety, and a union that is consulted early can settle both before a strike is called. Without a union, staff have no one to negotiate for them, which may make walkouts more likely. The deciding factor is whether management is willing to negotiate. Where it is not, no rule about unions will keep the trains running.

Two timing rules. Judge as you go: weigh each argument when you make it, then finish with a supported conclusion. And do not open with your verdict. A one-line verdict with no reasoning behind it earns at most 1 evaluation mark (principal examiner report, 9609/21, June 2024). Earn it, then state it.

What changes from paper to paper?

Paper 1: choose the essay by part (a)

Section A has four questions worth 5 marks each. Questions 1 to 3 are a 2-mark definition and a 3-mark explanation. Question 4 analyses one impact.

In Section B, choose your essay by part (a), not by the industry in part (b). If you cannot define and analyse the concept in part (a), take the other essay. Answer one essay only. If part (a) asks for two benefits, write two benefits and stop.

Paper 2: the data is the answer key

Two questions, 30 marks each. Each runs identify (1), explain (3), calculate (3), explain (3), analyse (8) and evaluate (12). Allow about 45 minutes a question, with roughly 12 on the 8-marker and 18 on the 12-marker.

Write the formula before the numbers. Label a variance favourable or adverse next to the figure. Part (c) has no evaluation marks, so skip the conclusion. In part (d), evaluate each point, then bring the points together at the end. The examiners are blunt about the data: candidates who ignore it are unlikely to gain any application marks (9609/22, June 2024).

Paper 3: read the case first, carry your number forward

Five questions on one case study. Questions 1 and 2 are 8 marks each, with no evaluation. Questions 3 and 4 are 16 marks each: calculations worth 4 marks, then a 12-mark evaluation. Question 5 is 12 marks.

Spend 10 to 15 minutes reading before you write. In the calculations, the syllabus formula for ARR divides average profit by average investment, and the unit and any negative sign are part of the answer. Carry your result into the 12-marker that follows. Cambridge's mark schemes apply an own-figure rule, so a wrong number that you interpret correctly still earns credit.

Paper 4: one question looks back, one looks forward

Two 20-markers on one case. Question 1 evaluates a strategy over the dates in the case. Question 2 advises on what to do next. Each is worth 3 knowledge, 2 application, 8 analysis and 7 evaluation marks.

Define the concepts you use first. The mark scheme says no application, analysis or evaluation marks can be awarded without knowledge. Plan for 15 minutes, then spend 30 on each question.

Do not walk down the timeline. Choose two or three strands, go deep, quote the figures and give a clear verdict. Then add a second evaluative point: what your verdict depends on. Our guide to the 20-mark business question goes further.

How should you spend the last week?

A boring week beats a clever one. Here is the order I would use. Plan the timed paper for when the power is on, because load-shedding has a sixth sense for the moment you open a past paper.

  1. Sit one timed paper in one go, for the paper you are taking. Past papers by paper and year are on the A Level Business 9609 past papers page.
  2. Mark it yourself with the mark scheme (our guide to using a mark scheme shows how). Tally the lost marks by skill: knowledge, application, analysis, evaluation. The column that lost the most marks is your revision plan.
  3. Fix your definitions. Each needs two clear parts. Four pairs trip students up every year: merger and takeover, job enrichment and job enlargement, market share and market size, efficiency and effectiveness. The full list is in the A Level Business definitions download.
  4. Rewrite three old conclusions with the judgement frame from the evaluation section. For a full-mark answer to compare against, the A Level Business Solved Past Papers ebook answers 24 papers in full from May/June and Oct/Nov 2025.
  5. Test yourself on the A Level Business (9609) quiz.
  6. Sleep the night before. Evaluation is the first thing a tired brain drops.

What to do next

  1. Know the splitCheck your paper's mark split before you write a word.
  2. Go deep, not wideDevelop two points fully. Do not list five.
  3. Use the casePut something from it in every paragraph: a product, a person or a figure.
  4. Give evaluation halfHalf of every 12-marker, with the judgement in the business's words.
  5. Mark by skillMark your own papers by skill, and revise the column that lost the most marks.
