Landing page copy, v1.2Answer the question before they ask it

A landing page sounds like a calm teacher who has seen this exact situation many times. It starts where the reader is, says plainly what we do, proves every claim with something specific, and makes the WhatsApp message take ten seconds. Warm in the body, dry in small doses, never hyped.

Foundations

Three tests for every line

1. Is it their situation?

Would a student or parent say "that is exactly me"? If the line could sit on any tuition centre's page, it fails.

2. Could a parent check it?

A number, a name, a paper, a time frame, a real review. "Best", "expert" and "quality" cannot be checked, so they are never used.

3. Does it move them?

It answers a question, removes a doubt or shows the method, one step closer to the WhatsApp message. A line that only decorates goes.

A line stays only if it passes all three.

Sell the want behind the want

Students do not buy tuition. They buy the moment the paper opens and it looks familiar. Parents do not buy classes. They buy the end of the worry. Write the feature, then the moment it creates.

  • One reader per section. Never both in the same paragraph.
  • The hero speaks to the student on A Level pages and to the parent on O Level pages.
  • Only the "For parents" block says "your child".

Principles

Seven things that make copy convert

  1. Start from their situationThe first line describes their problem in their words. "We" does not appear in the hero.
  2. Specific is believableA number, a paper, a code, a time frame, a named teacher doing a named thing.
  3. Every number has a baseline"250+ A*s in 8+ years", never "250+ A*s" alone.
  4. Feature and benefit in one breathJoin them with "so you" or "so your child".
  1. Answer objections in the openEach doubt gets its own visible answer. An answered objection becomes proof.
  2. Give something before you askOne real mistake explained, a marked answer, a free resource. The page-sized demo class.
  3. One idea at a timeEvery section works for someone who reads only the label and the tagline.

"You" appears at least twice as often as "we" on the whole page.

Mechanics

Label, tagline, two or three sentences

The problem

You know the content. The marks still leak.

Most Business students do not lose marks for knowing too little. They lose them for writing the wrong kind of answer. Writing more does not fix it, because examiners are not paid by the page.

The page

Eleven sections, one job each

  1. HeroThe page is about their exact problem. Teacher, timings, place and "from" fee in the card. One button. Avoid: "we", "best", a pun in the subheading.
  2. Proof strip3 or 4 checkable facts, each with a baseline. Rating linked to Google. Avoid: humour, numbers not in the register.
  3. The problemThe exact mark-losing mistakes, in subject terms and students' words. Avoid: a joke aimed at the reader.
  4. Method3 or 4 named steps. One scene and one hard fact each. Avoid: vague steps like "we focus on concepts".
  5. TeacherName, credentials with baselines, one named action, one real review line. Avoid: a link to the teachers page from subject pages.
  6. What you getEach feature joined to its benefit with "so you". Avoid: doubt support, a class-size number.
  7. For parentsCost, attention, progress, who is responsible. Plain and slow. Avoid: jokes about money.
  8. ReviewsThree, word for word, first name only, chosen by the proof ladder. Avoid: edited or invented quotes.
  9. Something usefulOne free resource that fits the page. Avoid: a hard sell next to it.
  10. FAQIn the order people ask. Optional dry line, then a concrete answer that stands alone. Avoid: a hidden fee.
  11. Final bandBack to their situation. One button. Avoid: urgency tricks.

Works

"Stop writing Business (9609) answers that lose marks." The reader's situation, a stance, and the keyword in the pill above it.

Does not work

"Secure A* in A Level Business with SJ Academy." It promises a grade and starts with us instead of them.

The H1 takes the stance. The subheading has none: it says plainly what the class is, who teaches it and where.

  1. Write 10 optionsFor the H1 and each tagline. The first one is almost never the best.
  2. Run the three testsMost options fail one. Cut them.
  3. Ask "so what?"Until the answer is something the reader feels. That answer is often the better line.
  4. Read the last three aloudPick the one you would say to a student across a desk.

Proof

The proof ladder

  1. Before and after, with a time frame"50 to 72 in past papers within 2 months."
  2. A named paper or grade"An A in A Level Maths."
  3. A named teacher doing a named thing"500+ past papers, solved on video."
  4. A physical or procedural detail"Every answer comes back marked against the mark scheme."
  5. The reader's type, servedPrivate candidates, late joiners, online students.
  6. Credentials with a baseline"12+ years, including at KGS."
  7. A rating with its count and a link"SJ Academy is rated 5.0 on Google from 226 reviews."

Never claim

  • A class-size number. Say "kept small" and show how attention works
  • One-on-one or home tuition
  • Demo classes for lab practicals
  • Doubt support outside class
  • Scheduled progress reports
  • A guaranteed grade, or any unconfirmed frequency
Not proof, and never used: best, top, leading, expert, experienced faculty, highly qualified, quality education, personalised attention, proven, guaranteed, affordable, state-of-the-art.

Turned down, never off

Works

"Practising the wrong structure 50 times makes you very good at the wrong structure." Then the concrete answer.

Does not work

A joke about the reader's current marks, about fees, or slang in a parent section.

Humour wraps around facts and never replaces them. A reader who skips every joke still gets every answer.

After it ships

Match the ad. Measure the page.

Ads and message match

  • One ad, one page. A Business ad lands on the Business page, never the homepage
  • The first screen repeats the ad's promise in the same words
  • An ad aimed at parents lands on a hero that speaks to parents
  • Every ad link tagged: utm_source, utm_campaign, utm_content

Measure and improve

  • WhatsApp clicks per 100 visits, by button (GA4)
  • Enquiries with the page code (lead tracker)
  • Search click-through rate (Search Console)
  • Questions people still ask become FAQ answers or hero lines
  • Change one thing at a time, H1 first, and give it 4 weeks

Before the page goes live

  • Page brief filled in, with the one big idea in one sentence
  • H1 chosen from at least 10 options
  • Every number is in the facts register, with a baseline
  • Reviews word for word, first name only, linked to Google
  • Nothing from the never-claim list
  • On a phone, the first screen shows subject, teacher, mode and button
  • All nine first-call questions answered somewhere
  • "From" fee matches the live fee page, with the link
  • WhatsApp message pre-filled, with the page reference
  • The hero starts from the reader, with no "we"
  • "You" outnumbers "we" two to one
  • Every feature joined to its benefit
  • No paragraph speaks to both student and parent
  • One dry line per section at most, none on facts, fees or buttons
  • No banned words, em dashes or exclamation marks
  • Old keywords kept in the pill, H1, subheading, one H2 and the first 100 words
  • The first screen matches any ad pointing here
  • SEO title and meta written, social title matches

Prompt block, for drafting with an AI model

Open the short prompt to paste

You are writing landing page copy for SJ Academy (sjacademy.pk), a Cambridge O and A Level tuition centre in DHA Phase 7, Karachi, with live online classes. The page has one job: get the right student to send a WhatsApp message booking a free demo class.

Page. [page and address], keywords to keep [list], lead reader for the hero [student / parent], teacher and approved claims [paste], real reviews [paste], class details [level, papers, series, mode].

Voice. A calm teacher who has seen this exact situation many times. Starts where the reader is, in their words. Specific, believable, warm, dry in small doses, never hyped. "You" twice as often as "we". No "we" in the hero.

Every line passes three tests: it is exactly the reader's situation, a sceptical parent could check it, and it moves them closer to the WhatsApp message.

Structure. Hero, proof strip, the problem, method, teacher, what you get, for parents, reviews, something useful, FAQ, final band. Each section: label, a 2 to 7 word tagline with a stance, then 2 or 3 sentences.

Proof. Only the facts provided, each with a baseline. Never a class size, one-on-one tuition, demos for practicals, doubt support outside class, scheduled reports or a guaranteed grade. Mark anything uncertain [confirm].

Style. Short sentences, active voice, British spelling, sentence case. No em dashes, no exclamation marks, no banned words. At most one dry line per section, never on facts, fees or buttons.
