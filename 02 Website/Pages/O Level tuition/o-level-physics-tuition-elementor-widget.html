The problem

Understood in class. Lost in the paper.

Most O Level Physics marks do not go on the hardest question. They go on small, fixable things, and usually in one of these four places.

The formula without the idea

Remembering speed = distance ÷ time is easy. Knowing which graph to read it from, and which part of the graph, is where the mark is.

The graph read the wrong way

On a speed-time graph, the gradient is acceleration and the area underneath is distance. Swap them and the whole part goes.

The unit left off

In the 5054 mark scheme's own words, a missing or incorrect unit will normally mean the final calculation mark is not awarded.

Describing, not explaining

“It gets colder” describes. “The fastest particles escape, so the average kinetic energy falls” explains. Only one of them scores.

None of this means your child is bad at Physics. These are habits, and habits change with practice that is marked properly.

how much of it? per kg1/2
Explanation · thermal physicsA cup of chai is left on the table. Explain, in terms of particles, why evaporation cools the chai. [2]
which ones? the fastest1/2

Pick a line on the answer above

The last line · 1 out of 2

The equation and the working earn the C1 mark. The A1 mark needs the unit, and the mark scheme says a missing unit will normally lose it. Full marks: E = 15 750 J, or 16 000 J to 2 significant figures.

The middle line · 1 out of 2

The definition never says how much of the substance. Specific heat capacity is per unit mass. Full marks: the energy needed to raise the temperature of 1 kg of the substance by 1 °C.

The first line · 1 out of 2

“Some” particles is the problem. The mark is for saying which ones: the particles with the most kinetic energy escape. Without that, the second line does not follow. Full marks: the most energetic particles escape, so the average kinetic energy of those left falls.

C1 = a working mark, given for a correct method. A1 = the answer mark, which needs its unit. B1 = an independent mark for one correct point. Sample questions written by SJ Academy in the style of 5054 Paper 2.

The syllabus

The O Level Physics syllabus (5054), paper by paper

O Level Physics covers six topics: motion, forces and energy; thermal physics; waves; electricity and magnetism; nuclear physics; and space physics. Every candidate sits Paper 1 and Paper 2, plus one practical paper: Paper 3 in a lab, or Paper 4 on paper.

Paper 1Multiple Choice

  • 1hduration
  • 40marks
  • 30%of the O Level

What it asks. 40 four-option questions across the whole syllabus.

How to prepare. About a minute and a half a question, so practise quick unit checks, sensible estimates and ruling out the option that is nearly right.

Paper 2Theory

  • 1h 45mduration
  • 80marks
  • 50%of the O Level

What it asks. Short-answer and structured questions: calculations, definitions, graphs and explanations. Half the O Level sits on this paper.

How to prepare. Working laid out for the C1 mark, a unit on every final answer, definitions with every key word, and explanations that give the reason, not just the result.

Paper 3Practical Test

  • 1h 30mduration
  • 40marks
  • 20%of the O Level

What it asks. Experiments done in a laboratory: taking readings, recording them in tables, plotting graphs and drawing conclusions.

How to prepare. Hands-on time with the apparatus, so the readings are quick and the table and graph are routine.

Paper 4Alternative to Practical

  • 1hduration
  • 40marks
  • 20%of the O Level

What it asks. The same practical skills as Paper 3, tested in writing. Candidates do not do experiments in this paper.

How to prepare. Readings to half the smallest scale division, units in table headings (time/s), significant figures that match the data, and a best-fit line on every graph.

84%

That is the weighted mark an A* needed in O Level Physics in June 2026: 168 out of 200, for candidates who sat Papers 12, 22 and 42. An A needed 137. The gap between them is made of single lost marks, like the ones in the answers above.

Are past papers and YouTube videos enough for O Level Physics?

They help, but videos explain the topic and do not mark your answer. Past papers show you the questions, not which line lost the mark. In the batch, answers are marked and you are told what to change. You can download the O Level Physics past papers free.

What is the difference between Paper 3 and Paper 4 in O Level Physics?

Both test the same practical skills and are each worth 20% of the O Level. Paper 3 is a Practical Test done in a laboratory. Paper 4, Alternative to Practical, is a written paper with no experiments. Candidates sit one of the two, alongside Papers 1 and 2.
