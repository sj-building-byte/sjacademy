The problem

Balanced in your head. Not on the page.

O Level Accounting is marked on method, layout and the right side of the ledger. Paper 2 is 70% of the grade, and these are the four places the marks usually go.

Debit and credit swapped

Money paid out of the bank is a credit in the business's own books. The bank statement shows the opposite, which is where the confusion starts.

Depreciation was treated as saving

Depreciation is a charge that spreads the cost of an asset over its useful life. It does not put cash aside to buy a new one.

The working was missing

Paper 2 is five structured questions of 20 marks each. A correct figure with no working earns less than a slightly wrong figure with clear method.

The layout lost marks

Statements, ledgers and journals have set formats with headings, dates and labels. A right total in the wrong layout still loses marks.

None of this means Accounting is not your subject. It means the method was not on the page. That part is fixable.

The last line

Paying by cheque reduces the bank balance in the business's own books, so the bank account is credited. The bank's statement shows it the other way round, and that is where the mix-up begins. Full marks: debit office equipment, credit bank.

The last line

Depreciation is an accounting charge that spreads the cost of the van over the years it is used. It does not set cash aside. The first line explained the reason, and the second line described something else.

The last line

Gross profit is revenue minus cost of sales: 90,000 - 55,000 = 35,000. The answer found the cost of sales and then did not use it, subtracting the closing inventory instead.

O Level Accounting has two compulsory papers: a multiple-choice paper worth 30% and a structured written paper worth 70%. Calculators are allowed in both. The syllabus has seven sections, from the fundamentals of accounting to accounting concepts and modern practice.
