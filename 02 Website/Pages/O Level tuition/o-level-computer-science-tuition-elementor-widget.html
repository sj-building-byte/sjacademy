The problem

Knew the topic. Wrote the wrong word.

O Level Computer Science is marked on exact words. An answer that is nearly right in your head is often worth nothing on paper. These are the four places the marks usually go.

Detect became correct

A parity check detects an error. It does not fix it. Swapping one verb for another can turn a full-mark answer into a zero.

What, not why

When a question says explain, it wants a reason. Describing what something does answers a different question.

The test data was wrong

Normal, abnormal, extreme and boundary data each mean something precise. Extreme data is accepted at the edge of the range, not just a big number.

The pseudocode drifted

Paper 2 is written on paper with no computer to catch mistakes, and it ends with a 15-mark scenario question that rewards clear, complete logic.

None of this means you can't do Computer Science. It means the examiner never saw what you knew. That part is fixable.

The last line

A parity check only tells the receiver that something is wrong. It cannot say which bit changed, so it cannot correct it. Full marks: the receiver counts the 1s in each byte and, if the count does not match the agreed parity, the data is flagged as an error and is sent again.

The last line

Extreme data is the largest or smallest value that should still be accepted, so here it is 1 or 100. 1000 is outside the range, which makes it abnormal data. Every term in this topic has a precise meaning, and the mark scheme holds you to it.

The last line

The first line is right, then the answer stops at better, which is not a reason. Explain means say why it matters for a laptop. Full marks: with no moving parts it is less likely to be damaged if the laptop is dropped or moved, and it uses less power, so the battery lasts longer.
