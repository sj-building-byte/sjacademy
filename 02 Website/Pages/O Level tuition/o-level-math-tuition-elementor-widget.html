The problem

Knew the method. Lost the marks.

Most O Level Maths marks are not lost on the hardest question. They go one at a time, on small things, and usually in one of these four places.

The working stayed in the head

A wrong answer with no working scores nothing. The same wrong answer with the method written down can still keep most of the marks.

The rounding came too early

Round halfway through and the final answer drifts. Cambridge wants non-exact answers to 3 significant figures, and angles to 1 decimal place.

The calculator was not allowed

Paper 1 is half the O Level, with no calculator. Fractions, percentages and standard form by hand cost time when they are not automatic.

The right angle, the wrong reason

Geometry questions often ask for a reason. “From the diagram” is not one. The theorem, named the way the mark scheme expects, is.

None of this means your child is bad at Maths. These are habits, and habits change with practice that is marked properly.

right angle, wrong theorem1/2

Pick a line on the answer above

The last line · 1 out of 2

The method is right and the value is right, but 8.1 is only 2 significant figures. Cambridge wants non-exact answers to 3 significant figures unless the question says otherwise, so the accuracy mark goes. Full marks: AC = 8.06 cm.

The last line · 1 out of 2

The factorisation earns the method mark, then a sign slips on the very last line. If x + 1 = 0, then x = −1, not 1. Full marks: x = 6 or x = −1.

The last line · 1 out of 2

The angle is right, but the reason names the wrong theorem, so the reason mark goes. Full marks: angle ABC = 70°, because the angle at the centre is twice the angle at the circumference.

M1 = a method mark, given for a correct method. B1 = an independent mark for one correct answer or statement. The last mark in each question here is for the final answer or the reason. Sample questions written by SJ Academy in the style of O Level Maths 4024.

The syllabus

The O Level Maths syllabus (4024), paper by paper

O Level Mathematics covers number, algebra and graphs, coordinate geometry, geometry, mensuration, trigonometry, transformations and vectors, probability and statistics. Every candidate sits two papers of equal weight: one without a calculator and one with.

Paper 1Non-calculator

  • 2hduration
  • 100marks
  • 50%of the O Level

What it asks. Short and structured questions from across the whole syllabus, with no calculator allowed. Fractions, percentages, standard form, algebra and geometry, all by hand.

How to prepare. Regular non-calculator practice, so the arithmetic stops costing time, and every step written down, so a slip still keeps the method marks.

Paper 2Calculator

  • 2hduration
  • 100marks
  • 50%of the O Level

What it asks. Structured and unstructured questions from across the whole syllabus, many with several steps. A scientific calculator is required.

How to prepare. Keep the full calculator value until the last line, give non-exact answers to 3 significant figures and angles to 1 decimal place, and lay out each step for the method marks.

173

That is the weighted mark out of 200 an A* needed in O Level Maths in June 2026, for candidates who sat Papers 12 and 22. An A needed 147. Across both papers, that leaves room for 27 lost marks, and most of them go one at a time, like the ones in the answers above.
