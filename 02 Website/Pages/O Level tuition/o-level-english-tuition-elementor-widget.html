The problem

Good English. Lost marks.

Most O Level English students are not short of vocabulary. The marks go on the rules of the paper: a summary that runs long, a letter that copies the text instead of using it, a composition that tells instead of shows. These are the four places they usually go.

The summary ran long

Paper 1 caps the summary at 150 words of continuous writing. Every word past the limit is a word the examiner did not need, and lifting whole sentences from Text B is not summarising.

The text was copied, not used

Directed writing gives you a 400 to 450 word text. Ten of its 25 marks are for reading: developing and evaluating those ideas, not repeating them in the same order.

The composition told, not showed

“It was a beautiful day” gives the examiner nothing to picture. Section B rewards detail, structure and sentences chosen for effect.

The language question named a device

Spotting a simile is the start. The marks in the use of language question are for explaining the effect the words create.

None of this means your child is weak at English. These are exam habits, and habits change with answers that are marked properly.

The last line

The first two lines are points. The last is an example, and examples and statistics are detail, not main points. It spends words the 150-word limit cannot spare and earns nothing. Full marks come from more benefits, each in your own words, with the examples left in the text.

The last line

This line repeats two ideas from the article without doing anything with them. Reading marks in directed writing are for developing and evaluating the text. Full marks: say whether the claim is fair and what should change, for example healthier options instead of closing it.

The last line

Two strong sentences, then one that tells the reader what to feel instead of showing it. In a descriptive composition, vague words like nice and beautiful hold the mark down. Full marks: replace it with something the reader can see, hear or smell.

Paper 1Reading

  • 2hduration
  • 50marks
  • 50%of the O Level

What it asks. Section A (25 marks) is comprehension and use of language on Text A, a narrative of about 900 words. Section B (25 marks) is a summary of Text B in no more than 150 words, plus a short response on implicit meaning.

How to prepare. Answer in your own words, explain effects rather than naming devices, and practise summaries against the clock and the word count.

Paper 2Writing

  • 2hduration
  • 50marks
  • 50%of the O Level

What it asks. Section A (25 marks) is directed writing: a speech, email, report, letter or article of 250 to 350 words built from one or two short texts. Section B (25 marks) is a composition of 350 to 450 words from four titles, two descriptive and two narrative.

How to prepare. Use the texts rather than copy them, match the register to the reader, and plan the composition before writing.

What is the word limit for the O Level English summary?

The Paper 1 summary must be continuous writing of no more than 150 words. Writing in note form, or going over the limit, costs marks. Summaries are practised in class against the word count.

How many words should an O Level English composition be?

About 350 to 450 words for the Section B composition, and about 250 to 350 words for the directed writing task in Section A of Paper 2.

How do I get an A* in O Level English?

Read the question for its purpose and audience, use the texts instead of copying them, stay inside the summary limit, and show rather than tell in your compositions. In class you practise exactly that on past paper questions, marked to the 1123 mark scheme.
