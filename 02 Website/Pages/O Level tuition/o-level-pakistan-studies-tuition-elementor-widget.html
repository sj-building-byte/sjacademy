The problem

Knew the facts. Missed the analysis.

O Level Pakistan Studies is marked on how you use knowledge, not how much of it you can write. Both papers carry equal weight, and these are the four places the marks usually go.

The source was ignored

Paper 1 Section A starts with source-based parts. An answer that does not use the source misses what the question is asking.

The 14-mark part was a description

Each Section B question ends with a 14-mark part that asks for analysis or evaluation, answered as an essay. Describing events fills the page but not the mark scheme.

Paper 2 answers had no places or figures

Paper 2 asks you to interpret resources, then analyse and evaluate. Environment answers need named places and figures, not general statements.

Three questions, ninety minutes

Paper 2 asks for three questions from five, about 30 minutes each. A first answer that runs long costs marks on the last.

None of this means you do not know Pakistan Studies. It means the examiner never saw the evidence. That part is fixable.

Parts (a) and (b) are source-based, so use the source in your answer. Parts (c) and (d) ask for more explanation and judgement.

Section B Two questions chosen from four50 marks

Each question is 25 marks in three parts: knowledge for 4 marks, understanding for 7 marks, and analysis and evaluation for 14 marks, answered in essay form. Most of the marks are in the last part.
