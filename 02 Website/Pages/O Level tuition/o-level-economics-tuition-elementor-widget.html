The problem

Knew the chapter. Missed the chain.

O Level Economics rewards the full line of reasoning, not just the right topic. Paper 2 is 70% of the grade, and these are the four places the marks usually go.

The definition was close

Definitions are marked on key words. Demand is not what people want. It is what they are willing and able to buy at a given price, and one missing word can cost the mark.

The curve moved the wrong way

A change in price moves you along a curve. A change in anything else shifts the whole curve. Mixing the two up turns a good explanation into a wrong one.

The chain stopped halfway

Explain means add the because. A statement such as costs rise earns the first mark, and the answer needs what happens next to earn the rest.

Section B ran out of time

Paper 2 has one compulsory 20-mark question and three more chosen from four, all in 2 hours. A first answer that runs long leaves too little for the last.

None of this means Economics is too hard. It means the examiner never saw the full chain. That part is fixable.

The last line

A movement along the demand curve happens only when the good's own price changes. A rise in income changes demand at every price, so the whole curve shifts. The first two lines already said so, and the last line contradicted them.

The last line

Opportunity cost is not the amount spent. Full marks need the next best alternative that is given up when a choice is made. The first line had the idea, and the second line replaced it with money spent.

The last line

Lower costs make production more profitable, so supply increases and output rises. The chain was right until the last line, where the direction flipped.
