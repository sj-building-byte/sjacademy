Updated 2 November 2026. Written for students sitting Cambridge O Level in Pakistan. Syllabus details are checked against Cambridge for the 2027 series.

Short answer: an O Level A* comes from method more than hours. Cambridge awards A* on your overall subject grade, using thresholds it publishes after each series, so there is no fixed percentage to aim for. The method we teach has four steps: learn the syllabus, practise on real past papers, mark them against the mark scheme, then fix every lost mark and test it again.

You can spend an evening highlighting notes, watching a lecture at 1.5x speed and ticking off a past paper, then lose marks on questions you thought you knew. The notes look great. The marks do not care.

That gap between studying and scoring is what this guide closes. It follows how we teach O Level in class, and it links to the free tools we use to do it.

A* is a subject grade, and the bar moves

Cambridge awards A* on the overall subject grade. It combines your components using the weightings in the syllabus. There is no A* for a single paper, so a great mark in one paper is good news, not a grade.

Cambridge also publishes grade thresholds after every series, and they change with how demanding the papers were. Here is the A* line for O Level Maths 4024 in June 2026.

A* started at 173 out of 200

Source: Cambridge Maths 4024 June 2026 grade threshold table. A past result, not a prediction.

Which pair applies depends on your exam entry, so ask your centre. Then compare your weighted total with that pair only. Never compare one paper with the overall line.

Every family has a relative who swears A* is always 90%. Cambridge publishes a table that disagrees.

For other subjects and series, use our O Level grade thresholds page.

One local detail: Cambridge does not issue Percentage Uniform Marks (PUM) in Pakistan, so do not expect one beside your grade.

Start with the syllabus, then learn what each paper looks like

The syllabus tells you what can be examined, which skills are tested and how much each component counts. Download the one for your exact subject code and exam year from Cambridge. Then turn its content list into a checklist and give every topic one of four labels.

Not started Learning Practised Secure

O Level is not written-only in every subject. The sciences have a practical component. In Physics 5054, Chemistry 5070 and Biology 5090 (2026 to 2028 syllabuses), a 40-mark Multiple Choice paper is worth 30%, an 80-mark Theory paper 50% and one practical assessment 20%. You sit Paper 3 (Practical Test) or Paper 4 (Alternative to Practical), not both. Ask your centre which route is yours.

What we tell students in each subject

Maths (4024)

Practise both formats. Paper 1 has no calculator, so speed and accurate mental arithmetic matter. Paper 2 rewards confident calculator use and clear working.

Take one diagnostic paper, sort every lost mark by topic, then start with the weakest topic. Cambridge does not publish a list of favourite topics, so do not guess one. For more, read our O Level Maths advice from a teacher.

Physics, Chemistry and Biology

Learn the practical skills your syllabus names: variables, apparatus, recording results, reading data, evaluating a method. Alternative to Practical is a written paper, but it still tests how experiments work.

In theory, use accurate terms. In calculations, show your method and units. In Chemistry, know the tests for ions and gases: Cambridge gives the qualitative analysis notes in the practical papers, not in Theory or Multiple Choice.

English Language (1123)

For Reading, find explicit and implied meaning and keep answers short. In a summary, pick the points that answer the question. Do not retell the passage.

For Directed Writing, fit the purpose, audience, form and register. For Composition, plan before you write. Ask a teacher for the one main reason an answer is not working yet, then fix that.

Business and Economics

Business: apply each idea to the case in front of you, build cause and effect, and make a decision you can defend. Learn the current formulas and ratios, and do not treat 7115 material as an exact match for 7081.

Economics: learn precise terms, read the data, build cause-and-effect chains and draw diagrams accurately. Evaluation needs reasons and context, not a generic last sentence.

Accounting (7707)

Practise double entry, adjustments, financial statements, incomplete records, journals and suspense accounts. Learn why each entry is made, not only the format. Practise explaining what the ratios show.

Computer Science (2210)

Reading about programming is like reading about swimming. You will know a lot about water. Write programs, trace algorithms by hand, test different inputs and debug your own mistakes.

For theory, learn precise technical explanations and apply them to the question. For 2027 exams, use the 2026 to 2028 syllabus.

Pakistan Studies (2059)

On the longer History answers, check which level your answer reaches, not only which facts you included, and use the sources in the insert. In Paper 2, bring figures from the maps and diagrams into your answer, and carry a ruler.

Read the question before you write the answer

A command word is an instruction. It is the one part of the question that tells you exactly what to do, which is rare enough in life that you should use it.

Command wordWhat it generally asks
StateGive a clear, direct fact or answer.
DescribeGive the relevant features, characteristics or pattern.
ExplainMake the reasons or the relationship clear, with support.
CompareIdentify relevant similarities and differences.
AnalyseExamine the information in detail and show how its parts connect.
Evaluate or assessMake a supported judgement about value, importance, quality or extent.

General meanings only. Your syllabus defines the exact command words for your subject.

The marks tell you how deep to go and how long to spend. They do not tell you how many sentences to write. Before you answer, find four things: the command word, the topic, the information you must use and the marks available. Then answer the task on the page, not everything you know about the topic.

Use precise subject words, and check the mark scheme when you lose credit. Alternative correct wording can score, so one missing word does not always mean zero. But a phrase you memorised without understanding will not survive an unfamiliar question.

Show clear working in Maths and science calculations. It earns method marks where the scheme gives them. Read the question too: the Physics 5054 specimen mark scheme gives full credit for a correct calculation answer without working, unless the question says to show it. A missing unit will normally cost the final mark. Every scheme prints its own notes, so read them.

The past-paper cycle we run in class

A past paper is not finished when you write the last line. It is finished when you have marked it, understood every lost mark, fixed it and checked later that you can now do it. A folder of downloaded papers is a folder.

  1. Learn the topic, then pull it out of your headStudy the idea, close the book and write what you can recall. Check the gaps, then do topical questions.
  2. Attempt it under exam conditionsCorrect time, no notes, no phone. Start with timed sections while you finish the syllabus, then move to full papers.
  3. Mark it against the official mark schemeGo line by line. Do not stop at the total. Write down why each mark went.
  4. Fix it and test it againRewrite the answer using the marking points. Revise the gap. Try a similar question a day or two later, without notes.

Our free past paper and mark scheme viewer opens the paper and its mark scheme side by side. Keep it on Don’t cheat: the mark scheme stays covered until you click “I’ve written my answer. Show the marks.” You can also find every paper by year and series on our O Level past papers page.

Pick papers that match your current syllabus. An older paper is fine for topical practice if the topic is still on the syllabus, but check its structure first. When the same series has an Examiner Report, read it after you mark. It describes common strengths and mistakes across candidates, not your script.

Keep an error log that changes what you do next

“Careless mistake” is not a diagnosis. Give every lost mark one of these reasons, then do the fix on the right.

Why the mark wentWhat you do next
Knowledge gap: a fact or concept you did not knowRelearn the topic, then recall it again without notes.
Misconception: an idea you understood wronglyRepair it with an explanation and a fresh example.
Question interpretation: you missed the command word or contextPractise naming the task before you write.
Application: you knew the idea but did not use it on the questionPractise with cases, data and scenarios.
Method or accuracy: wrong process, calculation, diagram or unitRedo the steps and check the conventions.
Time: a question left unfinished or rushedPractise timed sections and plan the minutes per mark.

Spread the work out. Short recall sessions across the week usually beat one marathon. A six-hour Sunday session feels heroic, and the topic you studied at hour one has left the building by Tuesday.

Eight habits that quietly cost marks

Rereading feels productive. It mostly feels familiar. Here are eight common habits, and the swap for each.

HabitDo this instead
Rereading without recallingClose the book, write what you remember, then check and repair the gaps.
Finishing papers without reviewing themMark the work, diagnose each lost mark and retest the weak skill.
Skipping Examiner ReportsRead the report for the series after you have attempted and marked the paper.
Skimming past command wordsName the task before you write a word.
Hiding your workingShow clear working and follow what the question asks.
Memorising answers you do not understandExplain why the answer is right, then use the idea in a new context.
Using vague termsLearn the definitions and use subject language accurately.
Keeping no record of mistakesKeep the error log and let it pick your next revision task.

These are common habits, not a diagnosis for every B. Your gap might be knowledge, foundations, confidence, attendance, feedback or time. Find the real problem before you pick the fix.

A weekly rhythm that survives eight subjects

You do not need two hours on every subject every day. You do need to stop letting a subject vanish for weeks. Start here, then adjust it around your timetable.

Weekly blockWhat to doWhy
Short subject touchpointsA 20 to 30 minute recall or question session for each subject, every week.Keeps earlier learning active.
Two priority sessionsPick your two weakest topics or skills. Spend 35 to 50 minutes on each, with practice questions.Gives extra time to the gaps that cost most.
One timed practiceA timed section, or a full paper if you have covered the syllabus.Builds exam technique without rushing into full papers.
One correction and retest blockMark the work, update the error log and retry one or two weak questions after a gap.Makes practice change what you can do next time.

In a busy school week, shorten the sessions. Do not drop the routine. The UPS gives you about 40 minutes. Use them on the question you have been avoiding.

Plan backwards from your exam date

  • Foundation: build knowledge with the syllabus checklist, recall and topical questions.
  • Consolidation: finish the syllabus, revisit earlier topics, start mixed questions and timed sections.
  • Exam practice: current-format papers, official mark schemes, Examiner Reports and the error log.
  • Final preparation: targeted practice. Alternate timed work with correction and retesting, and leave time to review every mock.
  • Exam period: short recall sessions, a second look at your key errors, and sleep.

Do not set a weekly paper quota before you know what you can mark. Eight subjects with one paper each a fortnight is already four papers a week to mark and correct. Your start date for timed work depends on how much of the syllabus you have covered, not on a universal January deadline.

Your first seven days

Do not reorganise your whole life tonight. Do one week. By day 7 you will have a syllabus checklist, a diagnosed weakness, a corrected answer and a plan for what to practise next.

  1. Collect your syllabusesGet the current syllabus for each subject. Confirm the code and exam year with your school or centre. You end the day with: A reliable subject list and checklist.
  2. Pick three weak spotsChoose one subject. Name three topics or skills you find hard, using recent classwork or a few diagnostic questions. You end the day with: Three specific priorities, not “revise more”.
  3. Recall and practise one topicWrite what you remember of one priority topic, check the gaps, then do topical questions. You end the day with: Clearer understanding and a set of practice answers.
  4. Attempt a timed sectionOpen the past paper viewer and do a timed section, with the mark scheme covered. You end the day with: A realistic picture of how you perform against the clock.
  5. Mark it and log every lost markMark it with the official scheme. Write down each lost mark and why it went. You end the day with: An error log with next actions.
  6. Correct and repairCorrect the answers. Relearn the idea behind each error and try a similar question. You end the day with: A corrected answer and a repaired gap.
  7. Retest and plan next weekRetest one or two weak areas without notes. Review the week and choose next week’s priorities. You end the day with: Proof of what improved, and what is left.

Do this next

  1. Get the syllabus for each subject and confirm the code with your centre.
  2. Do one timed section this week and mark it line by line.
  3. Start the error log, and retest the weakest question in two days.
FAQ

Questions about getting an A* at O Level

Is there a fixed percentage for an A* at O Level?
No. Cambridge publishes grade thresholds after each series and they change. In June 2026, the A* threshold for Maths 4024 was 173 out of 200 for components 12 and 22, and 175 out of 200 for components 13 and 23.
How many past papers should I do?
There is no magic number, and Cambridge sets no quota. A few papers that you have marked, corrected and retested teach more than a pile you have only attempted.
Which past papers should I use?
Use papers that match your current syllabus code and paper structure. Our past paper viewer has O Level papers from 2020 onwards, each beside its mark scheme. Business students should remember that papers up to 2026 are 7115, and the 7081 syllabus starts from June 2027.
Will I get a PUM in Pakistan?
No. Cambridge does not issue Percentage Uniform Marks in Pakistan or the Middle East and North Africa region.
