The problem

Interesting answer. No marks.

Sociology feels familiar because it is about families, schools and the media, things everyone has an opinion on. That familiarity is where the marks go. These are the four usual places.

Opinion instead of evidence

Your own family is not sociological evidence. Answers need concepts, studies, trends and theories.

Strength without the reason

“Questionnaires are easy” is not evaluation. The mark is for linking it to reliability, validity or representativeness.

Two sides, no judgement

Every essay is marked on analysis and evaluation. Ending with “both views have good points” leaves the top band empty.

The 26-mark essay ran out of time

Papers 1 and 2 each end with a 26-mark essay, nearly half the paper. Students who spend too long on short answers rush the part that matters most.

None of this means your child is bad at Sociology. These are writing habits, and they change with essays that are marked properly.

The last line

The first two lines evaluate. This one makes a claim without saying why it matters. Full marks: standardised questions can be repeated by another researcher in the same way, which makes the findings more reliable.

The last line

Personal experience is not sociological material. Extended families are a valid point, but they need evidence behind them. Full marks: use trends, studies or a postmodernist view of family diversity instead of one household.

The last line

Two views are explained, then the essay refuses to judge between them. The top band needs a reasoned conclusion. Full marks: say which view is more convincing and why, for example that the Marxist view explains the family's economic role but ignores the benefits feminists and functionalists point to.

What it asks. Section A has three compulsory questions. Section B is one 26-mark essay from a choice of two. It is 50% of a standalone AS.

How to prepare. Learn the methods with their strengths and limitations, and practise arguing against a view using sociological material.

Paper 2 · AS LevelThe Family

  • 1h 30mduration
  • 60marks
  • 25%of the A Level

What it asks. The same structure as Paper 1: three compulsory questions, then one 26-mark essay from a choice of two. It is 50% of a standalone AS.

How to prepare. Know the functionalist, Marxist, feminist, New Right and postmodernist views, and the trends behind family diversity.

Paper 3 · A2 LevelEducation

  • 1h 15mduration
  • 50marks
  • 21%of the A Level

What it asks. Four compulsory questions worth increasing marks, ending with a 26-mark essay.

How to prepare. Theories of education, social mobility and the curriculum, and how class, ethnicity and gender affect attainment.

Paper 4 · A2 LevelGlobalisation, Media and Religion

  • 1h 45mduration
  • 70marks
  • 29%of the A Level

What it asks. Two essays of 35 marks each, chosen from two different sections: globalisation, media and religion.

How to prepare. Pick two sections early and build deep evidence for both. Each essay must weigh contrasting views.
