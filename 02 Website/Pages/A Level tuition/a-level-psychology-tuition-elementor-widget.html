The problem

Knew the study. Lost the evaluation.

A Level Psychology students usually know the core studies well. The marks go on what happens next. These are the four places they usually go.

Reliability became generalisability

The papers mark these terms precisely. Saying a study is unreliable when you mean its sample was narrow costs the mark.

The finding was too vague

“Children copied the adult” describes the idea, not the finding. Results need detail: which children, which behaviour, compared with whom.

“Make it fair”

Research methods questions want the actual control: the same instructions, the same room, the same timing. Fair is not a method.

The study wasn't applied

Many questions give you a scenario. Answers that describe the study without using it for the scenario stay in the lower marks.

None of this means your child is bad at Psychology. It is precision, and precision improves with answers that are marked properly.

The last line

The first line is a good start. The second just restates it vaguely instead of completing the finding. Full marks need the comparison: more imitative aggression than children in the non-aggressive or control groups, with boys showing more imitative physical aggression than girls.

The last line

The evidence is about who the participants were, which is generalisability. The conclusion then names reliability, which is about consistency. Full marks: so the findings may not generalise to people who are not tired, or to people outside that panel.

The last line

Fair is not a reason the mark scheme rewards. The second mark is for explaining what the control does. Full marks: so that noise cannot affect some participants' recall more than others, which would be an extraneous variable.
