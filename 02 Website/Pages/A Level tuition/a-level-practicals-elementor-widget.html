The problem

You know the theory. The lab is different.

Paper 3 marks are rarely lost on the science. They go on the table heading, the decimal places, the observation that stops one step short, the drawing that looks like art instead of biology. Examiners mark what is on the page, the same way every time.

Physics · sample results tableRecord your readings of length and time.

The table loses marks first

Every column heading needs the quantity and its unit, like length / cm. Every raw reading in a column needs the same number of decimal places, matching the instrument you used.

Chemistry · sample observationAdd NaOH(aq) to the solution, then add it in excess.
white ppt
and in excess? dissolves or not?

The observation stops early

Qualitative analysis marks need the full observation: the colour, the precipitate, and what happens when more reagent is added. Half an observation gets half a chance.

The drawing looks like art

Biology drawings are marked on rules: large, sharp pencil, single clear lines, no shading, and label lines that are ruled and do not cross.

None of this is talent. These are lab habits, and habits come from doing the experiment, getting it checked and doing it again.

How a practical runs

Do it. Record it. Get it checked.

Every lab session follows the same four steps, so by your exam the routine is automatic and the paper feels like another Saturday.

  1. Set it up yourselfYou build the circuit, fill the burette, focus the microscope. The teacher guides; your hands do the work. 20+ Cambridge-style experiments per subject.
  2. Record it the Cambridge wayTables, graphs, significant figures, units, errors and conclusions, written the way the mark scheme reads them. The practical manual is provided.
  3. Get every practical checkedYour teacher checks each practical and marks the corrections on the page, so you know which line would have lost the mark.
  4. Rehearse before the paperOnce the core skills are covered, you come back before your exam for final revision and timed practice.

What Paper 3 asks. Two 20-mark experiments. One ends in a graph and a conclusion. The other has a deliberately weak method that you evaluate and improve.

What you practise. Reading instruments to the right precision, tables with units in the headings, graphs that fill the grid, and improvements specific enough to earn the mark.

  • Oscillations and simple pendulums
  • Objects in equilibrium
  • Springs, Hooke's law and force against extension
  • Density measurements
  • Resistance and resistivity of a wire
  • Filament lamp I-V characteristics
  • Electrical circuits with ammeters and voltmeters
  • Graph analysis: gradient, intercept and the constant
  • Errors, uncertainties and improvements

Chemistry · Sir Salman JunaidA Level Chemistry practicals (9701 Paper 3)

  • 2hduration

What Paper 3 asks. Usually a titration or other quantitative experiment, a second experiment such as enthalpy or rates, and qualitative analysis of unknown substances.

What you practise. Burette readings to 0.05 cm3, concordant titres, accurate observations for every test, and conclusions that follow from what you actually saw.

  • Acid-base titrations
  • Redox titrations
  • Water of crystallisation
  • Enthalpy change experiments
  • Rate of reaction experiments
  • Qualitative analysis of cations and anions
  • Tests for gases
  • Organic tests for functional groups
  • Salt analysis and planning questions

Free, before you join

A Level practical tips examiners check

Nine rules that cost students marks every series, across Physics, Chemistry and Biology. Use them on your next past paper, with or without us.

  1. Headings carry the unitEvery column heading has the quantity and its unit, like length / cm or time / s.
  2. One precision per columnRaw readings in a column have the same number of decimal places, matching the instrument: a metre rule to 0.1 cm, a burette to 0.05 cm3.
  3. Fill the gridChoose graph scales so your plotted points cover more than half the grid in both directions, with no awkward steps like 3 or 7.
  4. Plot sharp, draw thinSmall crosses or dots in circles, and one thin best-fit line with the points balanced on either side.
  5. Big gradient triangleDraw the triangle across at least half the length of the line, and quote the points you used.
  6. Match the significant figuresCalculated values are given to 2 or 3 significant figures, in line with the raw data they came from.
  7. Finish the observationIn Chemistry, write the colour, the precipitate and what happens in excess, not just “white ppt”.
  8. Draw biology, not artSharp pencil, single clean lines, no shading, ruled label lines that never cross.
  9. Make improvements specificSay exactly what to change and why it helps. “Be more careful” and “repeat it” on their own rarely earn the mark.

Questions about A Level practicals

What are the practical centre fees?

Rs. 25,500 per subject, paid once. That covers the full practical course for one exam series, with no monthly lab charge. New students also pay the one-time admission fee. Full figures are on the fee structure page.

Is there an A Level practical centre near me in Karachi?

SJ Academy's lab is at Plot 29C, Lane 5, Seher Commercial Area, DHA Phase 7, Karachi, close to Clifton and the rest of DHA. Students come from across Karachi for the Saturday lab sessions. Get directions.

Can private candidates join the A Level practicals?

Yes. Private candidates join the same lab sessions as school students, whether it is their first practical exam or a retake. You get the same experiments, the same practical manual and the same checking.

When should I join, and is it too late?

The best time is the start of the session, because you get time to do each practical slowly, fix your mistakes and do it again. Joining later still works: weekday slots open closer to the exams, and you may need to come more often to cover everything. The fee is the same either way.

Can I join just before my practical exam?

Yes. The practical workshop runs before each exam series and covers every experiment on the list in 5 to 7 days, in the same lab with the same teachers. The fee is Rs. 25,500 per subject, the same as the full course. If you have never done a practical before, join the full course earlier if you can. Practice is the part that cannot be rushed.

Do full course students pay extra for the workshop?

No. If you are in the full practical course, you join the pre-exam workshop at no extra cost.

Is there a demo class for lab practicals?

No. A lab session is the course itself, so practical students join directly. Message us on WhatsApp with your subject and exam session and we will tell you where the batch is and when your first lab is.

Which practicals do you cover?

20+ Cambridge-style experiments per subject, covering every skill Paper 3 tests. Physics covers oscillations, equilibrium, springs, resistance and circuits. Chemistry covers titrations, enthalpy, rates and qualitative analysis. Biology covers microscopy, drawings, enzymes, osmosis and food tests. The full list is above.

What do I need to bring?

Basic stationery, a calculator and a 30 cm ruler. These can also be bought at the academy. Keep a lab coat for Chemistry and Biology. SJ Academy provides the practical manual.

Can I do A Level lab practicals online?

No. Paper 3 is a hands-on exam, so the practicals happen in our lab in Karachi. Theory classes for Physics, Chemistry and Biology do run live online.

Do you cover Paper 5 as well?

Paper 5 is a written paper with no lab, so the lab sessions focus on Paper 3. Planning and data questions are part of the Chemistry and Biology practical lists, and A2 Physics students get a separate Paper 5 workshop in their theory course.

Do you run A Level practical mock exams?

Yes. Before each exam series we run timed practical mocks in a real exam set-up, at Rs. 4,000 per subject. When a mock is announced, it is open to everyone, including students from other schools and academies. Ask on WhatsApp for the next one.
