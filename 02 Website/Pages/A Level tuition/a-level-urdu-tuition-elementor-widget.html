The problem

Fluent Urdu. Capped marks.

A Level Urdu students are usually strong in the language. The marks go on the rules of each paper, and a few of them are strict. These are the four places they usually go.

The texts answer ran past 600 words

Cambridge advises 500 to 600 words for each Paper 4 answer. Write more and that answer cannot be placed above the 16 to 17 band out of 25.

The essay was all content

Paper 3 gives 24 of its 40 marks for the quality of the language and 16 for content. Ideas alone cannot carry it.

The answer retold the story

Paper 4 rewards understanding of how a text works and what the author is saying, not a summary of the plot.

The response forgot the opinion

The last Paper 2 task draws on both passages and adds your own view. Leaving out the personal response leaves marks on the table.

None of this means your child's Urdu is weak. These are exam rules, and they are easy to learn once someone marks against them.

Paper 2Reading and Writing

  • 1h 45mduration
  • 70marks
  • c39%of the A Level

What it asks. Two related passages, together no more than 750 words, with vocabulary, grammar and comprehension questions, then a response of about 140 words drawing on both passages and your own opinion.

How to prepare. Answer precisely, keep your Urdu accurate, and plan the final response so both passages and your view are in it.

Paper 3Essay

  • 1h 30mduration
  • 40marks
  • c22%of the A Level

What it asks. One essay of 250 to 400 words from five questions, one on each of the year's set topics.

How to prepare. Prepare all five topics, and write in varied, accurate Urdu, because language carries 24 of the 40 marks.

Paper 4Texts

  • 2h 30mduration
  • 75marks
  • 39%of the A Level

What it asks. Three answers, each on a different set text, with at least one from each section of the list. Each answer is worth 25 marks.

How to prepare. Know the texts in detail, stay within 500 to 600 words, and write about meaning and the author's intention.
