The problem

A Level Maths is marked on your working

Most students start A Level Maths believing the job is to reach the right answer. Cambridge mark schemes care just as much about how you got there. The examiner cannot give marks for what happened in your head.

The working is missing

Method marks are given for the steps. Skip the steps and there is nothing to mark, even when the final answer is right.

The rounding came early

Round in the middle of a question and the final answer drifts. Cambridge wants non-exact answers to 3 s.f. and angles in degrees to 1 decimal place.

The answer was already given

On a "show that" question the answer is printed for you. Every mark is for the steps that reach it, so one jump in the algebra costs marks.

The form was wrong

Exact form, in terms of π, as a fraction, as an inequality. A correct value in the wrong form still loses the last mark.

The method

Read a mark scheme like an examiner

Every mark in a 9709 mark scheme carries a letter. Once you know what each letter pays for, you know what to write down. This is the first thing Sir Salman teaches, and every test after it is marked the same way.

Pick a mark type

M is a method mark

Given for using a valid method on the problem. A slip in the arithmetic does not lose it. Skipping the step does.

A is an accuracy mark

Given for a correct answer or a correct step. It depends on the method mark before it, so no method shown usually means no accuracy mark either.

B is an independent mark

Given for a correct result or statement on its own, with no method mark attached. One line, one mark.

FT means follow through

If an earlier answer was wrong but you used it correctly afterwards, the later marks can still be given. So you keep going after a mistake. You do not cross out the page.

AG means answer given

The answer is printed in the question, usually after "show that". The marks are for every step that gets there, and examiners check that working closely.

The syllabus

The A Level Maths syllabus (9709), paper by paper

AS Level Maths is Pure 1 with one applied paper. The full A Level adds Pure 3 and a second applied paper. The two Pure papers carry 60% of the A Level.

PaperWhat it coversLengthMarks
Paper 1: Pure 1 (P1) AS LevelQuadratics, functions, coordinate geometry, circular measure, trigonometry, series, differentiation and integration1 hour 50 minutes75
Paper 3: Pure 3 (P3) A2 LevelAlgebra, logarithms and exponentials, trigonometry, differentiation, integration, numerical solutions, vectors, differential equations and complex numbers1 hour 50 minutes75
Paper 4: Mechanics (M1)Forces and equilibrium, kinematics, momentum, Newton's laws of motion, and energy, work and power1 hour 15 minutes50
Paper 5: Statistics 1 (S1)Representation of data, permutations and combinations, probability, discrete random variables and the normal distribution1 hour 15 minutes50
Paper 6: Statistics 2 (S2)The Poisson distribution, linear combinations of random variables, continuous random variables, sampling and estimation, and hypothesis tests1 hour 15 minutes50
