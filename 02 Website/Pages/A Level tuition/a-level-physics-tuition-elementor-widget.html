The problem

You did the physics. The mark still went.

Most A Level Physics students do not lose marks because the physics beat them. They lose them on the last line: the unit left off, the one word missing from a definition, the explanation that jumps to the answer. Examiners are not mind readers. They mark what is written.

Sample answer lineCalculate the charge that passes. [2]
Q = It = 0.030 × 240 = 7.2
7.2 what? no unit, no A1

The unit was missing

In a 9702 mark scheme, a missing or wrong unit costs the final answer mark, unless the question gives the unit its own mark. Right physics, one mark lighter.

Sample answer lineDefine the moment of a force. [1]
Moment = force × distance
which distance? perpendicular

The definition lost a word

The May/June 2025 mark scheme wanted force × perpendicular distance from the point. Drop “perpendicular” and the only mark on offer is gone.

Sample answer lineExplain why the current in the thermistor increases as it warms up. [2]
Because the temperature goes up.
and the resistance does what?

The explanation skipped a step

“Explain” marks sit in the chain: temperature rises, so resistance falls, so current rises. Repeat the question back and there is nothing to mark.

None of these are gaps in understanding. They are habits, and habits can be fixed with practice that is marked properly.

The method

Mark it like an examiner

A real 2-mark question from May/June 2025, answered three ways. Same physics, different marks. Learning why is the first thing Sir Salman teaches, and every test after it is marked the same way.

tap an answer to mark it

Pick an answer

Answer A · 0 out of 2

The time was left in minutes. The C1 mark needs the time in seconds (4.0 × 60), so there is no working mark to give, and the answer is wrong too.

Answer B · 1 out of 2

The physics is perfect, so the C1 mark is given. The A1 mark needs the unit, and a missing unit costs the final mark.

Answer C · 2 out of 2

Same working as Answer B, one letter longer. Both marks.

C = compensatory: given for correct working, and given automatically when the final answer is right. A = the answer mark, which needs its unit. B = an independent mark. M = a method mark that later A marks depend on. Based on the Cambridge 9702 mark scheme, May/June 2025.

The syllabus

The A Level Physics syllabus (9702), paper by paper

AS Physics covers physical quantities and units, kinematics, dynamics, forces, density and pressure, work, energy and power, deformation of solids, waves and superposition, electricity, DC circuits and particle physics. A2 adds motion in a circle, gravitational and electric fields, ideal gases and thermodynamics, oscillations, capacitance, magnetic fields, alternating currents, quantum and nuclear physics, medical physics, and astronomy and cosmology.

AS students sit Papers 1, 2 and 3. The full A Level adds Papers 4 and 5.

Paper 1 · AS LevelMultiple Choice

  • 1h 15mduration
  • 40marks
  • 15.5%of the A Level

What it asks. 40 four-option questions on the AS content. It is 31% of a standalone AS.

What you practise. Under two minutes a question, quick unit checks and estimates, and ruling out the option that is nearly right.

Paper 2 · AS LevelAS Structured Questions

  • 1h 15mduration
  • 60marks
  • 23%of the A Level

What it asks. Structured questions on the AS content: definitions, derivations, calculations and short explanations. It is 46% of a standalone AS, the biggest single paper at AS.

What you practise. Definitions word for word, working laid out for C and A marks, units and significant figures on every final answer.

Paper 3 · AS LevelAdvanced Practical Skills

  • 2hduration
  • 40marks
  • 11.5%of the A Level

What it asks. Two 20-mark experiments in the lab. One ends in a graph and a conclusion. The other has a deliberately weak method that you evaluate and improve. It is 23% of a standalone AS.

Where we teach it. In the A Level lab practicals, with real apparatus. Practical students join directly; there is no demo class for practicals.

Paper 4 · A2 LevelA Level Structured Questions

  • 2hduration
  • 100marks
  • 38.5%of the A Level

What it asks. Structured questions on the A2 content, with the AS content assumed. The heaviest paper in 9702 Physics.

What you practise. Long multi-step calculations, fields and capacitors questions under time, and explanations in quantum and nuclear physics that earn every mark.

Paper 5 · A2 LevelPlanning, Analysis and Evaluation

  • 1h 15mduration
  • 30marks
  • 11.5%of the A Level

What it asks. Two 15-mark written questions, no lab. Plan an experiment with a diagram, then use data and an equation to find a constant and its uncertainty.

Where we teach it. A separate 5-day Paper 5 workshop in May: variables and controls, graph analysis, uncertainties, and limitations and improvements.

How do I get an A* in A Level Physics (9702)?

Understanding the physics is the starting point, not the finish. Examiners give the final mark only with the correct unit, give definition marks only for the exact wording, and give explanation marks for each step of cause and effect. In class you practise past paper questions marked to the 9702 mark scheme until those habits are automatic.

Are A Level Physics past papers enough to prepare?

On their own, no. Past papers and videos show you the questions. They do not mark your answer or tell you which line lost the mark. In the batch, past paper answers are marked and you are told what to change. You can download the A Level Physics past papers free.

What is the difference between AS Level Physics and A2 Physics?

AS Level is Papers 1, 2 and 3: multiple choice, structured questions and a practical exam on the AS content. A2 adds Paper 4, structured questions on the A Level content, and Paper 5, planning, analysis and evaluation. We teach the full 9702 syllabus for both.

Can private candidates join the A Level Physics class?

Yes. Private candidates join the same batch as school students, with the same tests, mocks and past paper practice.
