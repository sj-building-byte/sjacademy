The problem

Right reaction. Wrong words.

Most A Level Chemistry students do not lose marks because the chemistry beat them. They lose them on wording: a definition one word short, an equation that answers a slightly different question, an explanation that names the result but not the reason. The mark scheme is a list of exact points, and the examiner ticks only what is on the page.

Sample answer lineDefine the term first ionisation energy. [2]
Energy needed to remove one electron from each atom in one mole of atoms.
atoms of what state? gaseous

The definition dropped a word

The 9701 mark scheme wants one electron removed from each atom in one mole of gaseous atoms. Leave out “gaseous” and the second mark goes with it.

Sample answer lineWrite an equation, with state symbols, for the second ionisation energy of calcium. [1]
Ca(g) → Ca2+(g) + 2e−
that is two steps, not the second

The equation answered another question

The second ionisation energy starts from Ca+(g), so the mark is for Ca+(g) → Ca2+(g) + e−. Removing both electrons at once is a different process.

Sample answer lineExplain why the boiling points of the halogens increase down Group 17. [2]
Because the molecules get bigger.
bigger how? which forces?

The explanation skipped the forces

The marks sit in the chain: more electrons, so stronger instantaneous dipole-induced dipole forces, so more energy is needed to overcome them. “Bigger” names none of it.

None of these are gaps in understanding. They are habits, and habits can be fixed with practice that is marked properly.

The method

Four points. Two marks.

A real 2-mark definition from May/June 2022, answered three ways. The mark scheme lists four points: two or three of them earn 1 mark, all four earn 2. Every answer in class is marked the same way, point by point, so you see exactly which word was missing.

The syllabus

The A Level Chemistry syllabus (9701), paper by paper

AS Chemistry covers atomic structure, moles and stoichiometry, chemical bonding, states of matter, energetics, electrochemistry, equilibria and reaction kinetics; the Periodic Table, Group 2, Group 17, and nitrogen and sulfur; and organic chemistry from hydrocarbons to carboxylic acids, nitrogen compounds, polymers, synthesis and analytical techniques. A2 goes deeper into energetics, electrochemistry, equilibria and kinetics, adds the transition elements, and extends organic chemistry and analysis, including NMR.

AS students sit Papers 1, 2 and 3. The full A Level adds Papers 4 and 5.

Paper 1 · AS LevelMultiple Choice

  • 1h 15mduration
  • 40marks
  • 15.5%of the A Level

What it asks. 40 multiple choice questions on the AS content. It is 31% of a standalone AS.

What you practise. Under two minutes a question, quick mole calculations, and ruling out the option that is nearly right.

Paper 2 · AS LevelAS Level Structured Questions

  • 1h 15mduration
  • 60marks
  • 23%of the A Level

What it asks. Structured questions on the AS content: definitions, equations, calculations, mechanisms and short explanations. It is 46% of a standalone AS, the biggest single paper at AS.

What you practise. Definitions point by point, equations with state symbols, working laid out step by step, and answers to a sensible number of significant figures.

Paper 3 · AS LevelAdvanced Practical Skills

  • 2hduration
  • 40marks
  • 11.5%of the A Level

What it asks. Practical work in the lab, with structured questions on practical skills. It is 23% of a standalone AS.

Where we teach it. In the A Level lab practicals, with real apparatus, taught by Sir Salman Junaid. Practical students join directly; there is no demo class for practicals.

Paper 4 · A2 LevelA Level Structured Questions

  • 2hduration
  • 100marks
  • 38.5%of the A Level

What it asks. Structured questions on the A2 content, with the AS content assumed. The heaviest paper in 9701 Chemistry.

What you practise. Long multi-step questions on equilibria, electrochemistry and transition elements, organic synthesis routes, and reading NMR and other analytical data.

Paper 5 · A2 LevelPlanning, Analysis and Evaluation

  • 1h 15mduration
  • 30marks
  • 11.5%of the A Level

What it asks. Written questions, no lab: plan an experiment, process data, and evaluate the method. The context can come from outside the syllabus.

Where we teach it. A separate Paper 5 workshop in May: planning experiments, variables and controls, graph analysis, uncertainties and evaluating data.

How do I get an A* in A Level Chemistry (9701)?

Understanding the chemistry is the starting point, not the finish. Mark schemes give definition marks only when every point is there, give equation marks only with the right species and state symbols, and give explanation marks for each step from structure to property. In class you practise past paper questions marked to the 9701 mark scheme until that wording is automatic.

Are A Level Chemistry past papers enough to prepare?

On their own, no. Past papers show you the questions. They do not mark your answer or tell you which word lost the mark. In the batch, past paper answers are marked and you are told what to change. You can download the A Level Chemistry past papers free.

What is the difference between AS and A Level Chemistry?

AS Level is Papers 1, 2 and 3: multiple choice, structured questions and a practical exam on the AS content. The full A Level adds Paper 4, structured questions on the A2 content, and Paper 5, planning, analysis and evaluation. We teach the full 9701 syllabus for both.
