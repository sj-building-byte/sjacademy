The problem

You knew the biology. The word lost it.

A Level Biology is a vocabulary exam wearing a lab coat. Mark schemes credit precise terms, so an answer that means the right thing in the wrong words can score nothing. These are the swaps that cost students the most marks, and the ones Sir Umair corrects first.

  • You wroteThe enzyme is killed at high temperature.
    The mark scheme wantsThe enzyme is denatured: its active site changes shape, so the substrate no longer fits.
    enzymes were never alive
  • You wroteWater moves from a high concentration to a low concentration.
    The mark scheme wantsWater moves by osmosis from a higher water potential to a lower water potential, across a partially permeable membrane.
    concentration of what?
  • You wroteRespiration produces energy.
    The mark scheme wantsRespiration releases energy, which is used to make ATP.
    energy is never made
  • You wroteThe person breathes in infected droplets.
    The mark scheme wantsThe person breathes in airborne droplets carrying M. tuberculosis.
    droplets can't catch TB
  • You wroteAntibodies kill the bacteria.
    The mark scheme wantsAntibodies bind to antigens on the bacteria and cause agglutination, so phagocytes can engulf them.
    they tag. phagocytes eat.
  • You wroteThe rate goes up and then down.
    The mark scheme wantsThe rate rises to a peak of 2.4 au at 40 °C, then falls to 0 at 60 °C.
    quote the graph, with units

None of these are gaps in knowledge. They are habits of wording, and habits change with practice that is marked properly.

The method

Mark it like an examiner

A real 2-mark question from May/June 2025, answered three ways. Same idea, different marks. Learning why is the first thing Sir Umair teaches, and every test after it is marked the same way.

Pick an answer

Answer A · 0 out of 2

Crowding is why TB spreads faster, not how it is transmitted. There is no cough or sneeze, nobody breathes anything in, and there are no droplets. Nothing to credit.

Answer B · 1 out of 2

An infected person coughs and someone else breathes in, so the first marking point is given. “Infected droplets” is marked I, ignore: droplets are not infected, the bacteria are carried in them. The second mark goes.

Answer C · 2 out of 2

Same idea as Answer B, in the words the mark scheme asks for: droplets carrying the bacteria, and the term droplet infection. Both marks.

The syllabus

The A Level Biology syllabus (9700), paper by paper

AS Biology covers cell structure, biological molecules, enzymes, cell membranes and transport, the mitotic cell cycle, nucleic acids and protein synthesis, transport in plants, transport in mammals, gas exchange, infectious diseases and immunity. A2 adds energy and respiration, photosynthesis, homeostasis, control and coordination, inheritance, selection and evolution, classification, biodiversity and conservation, and genetic technology.

AS students sit Papers 1, 2 and 3. The full A Level adds Papers 4 and 5.

Paper 1 · AS LevelMultiple Choice

  • 1h 15mduration
  • 40marks
  • 15.5%of the A Level

What it asks. 40 four-option questions on the AS content. It is 31% of a standalone AS.

What you practise. Under two minutes a question, reading diagrams and data quickly, and ruling out the option that is almost the right term.

Paper 2 · AS LevelAS Structured Questions

  • 1h 15mduration
  • 60marks
  • 23%of the A Level

What it asks. Structured questions on the AS content: labelling, short explanations, data and graph questions, and the occasional calculation. It is 46% of a standalone AS, the biggest single paper at AS.

What you practise. Mark scheme vocabulary, one idea per marking point, and describing data with figures and units.

Paper 3 · AS LevelAdvanced Practical Skills

  • 2hduration
  • 40marks
  • 11.5%of the A Level

What it asks. Hands-on experiments in the lab: recording results in a table, drawing graphs, microscope work and biological drawings. It is 23% of a standalone AS.

Where we teach it. In the A Level lab practicals at DHA Phase 7, Karachi, on weekends, with real apparatus. Practical students join directly; there is no demo class for practicals.

Paper 4 · A2 LevelA Level Structured Questions

  • 2hduration
  • 100marks
  • 38.5%of the A Level

What it asks. Structured questions on the A2 content, with the AS content assumed. The heaviest paper in 9700 Biology.

What you practise. Long explanations in respiration, photosynthesis and homeostasis that earn every marking point, genetics problems, and applying ideas to unfamiliar data.

Paper 5 · A2 LevelPlanning, Analysis and Evaluation

  • 1h 15mduration
  • 30marks
  • 11.5%of the A Level

What it asks. Written questions, no lab. Plan an investigation, then analyse and evaluate data, including statistical tests.

Where we teach it. A separate Paper 5 workshop in May: variables and controls, graph interpretation, statistical tests and evaluation.

How do I get an A* in A Level Biology (9700)?

Knowing the biology is the starting point, not the finish. Examiners credit precise terms, so “denatured” scores where “killed” does not, and “water potential” scores where “concentration” does not. Describe questions want figures quoted with units, and explain questions want each step of the cause. In class you practise past paper questions marked to the 9700 mark scheme until that wording is automatic.

Are A Level Biology past papers enough to prepare?

On their own, no. Past papers and notes show you the questions. They do not mark your answer or tell you which word lost the mark. In the batch, past paper answers are marked and you are told what to change. You can download the A Level Biology past papers free.

What is the difference between AS Level Biology and A2 Biology?

AS Level is Papers 1, 2 and 3: multiple choice, structured questions and a practical exam on the AS content. A2 adds Paper 4, structured questions on the A Level content, and Paper 5, planning, analysis and evaluation. We teach the full 9700 syllabus for both.

Can private candidates join the A Level Biology class?
