The problem

Strong views. Weak marks.

General Paper looks like the easy subject because there is nothing to memorise. That is exactly why students lose marks in it. These are the four places they usually go.

The opening said nothing

Paper 1 is one essay of about 600 to 700 words. An opening paragraph about human history is words the essay cannot spare.

The example was vague

“Many people say” is not evidence. Specific, relevant examples, ideally from your own country, are what make an argument land.

The essay never answered the question

Ten titles, one choice. Writing about the topic instead of the exact question asked is the most common way to lose a whole band.

The comprehension was copied

Paper 2 has two sections of 25 marks. When a question asks for your own words, lifting the passage's phrasing earns nothing.

None of this means you can't write. It means the examiner needs an argument, not an opinion. That part is fixable.

The first two lines are a strong opening. This one is a generic opener that could start any essay on communication. It uses words a 600 to 700 word essay cannot spare and earns nothing. Cut it and start with the question.

The last line

After a specific example, this line falls back on a vague claim with no evidence behind it. General Paper rewards examples that are specific and relevant. Full marks: name a real case, or explain the effect on the people who use it.

The last line

The question says using your own words, and this line lifts the writer's phrase straight from the passage. Copied wording does not show understanding. Full marks: say it plainly, for example that the shade lowers temperatures where many people live close together.
