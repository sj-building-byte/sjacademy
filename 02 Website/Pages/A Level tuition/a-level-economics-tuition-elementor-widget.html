The problem

You know the theory. The marks still slip.

Most A Level Economics students do not lose marks for forgetting the theory. They lose them in what they write around it: the diagram nobody explains, the essay that argues one side, and the answer that ignores the data in front of it. Examiners mark what is on the page, not what you meant.

Sample answer lineThe effect of a subsidy on producers
[diagram drawn] As shown in the diagram, the price falls.
why did S shift? say it

Drawn, not explained

The mark scheme wants diagrams that are fully explained. A correct graph with no sentence about why the curve moved is half an answer.

Sample answer lineAssess whether a minimum wage reduces poverty
It raises incomes, so poverty falls. Therefore it is the best policy.
one side = 0 for evaluation

One side, no evaluation

On a 12-mark essay part, 4 marks are evaluation, and a one-sided answer gets none of them. A perfect case for one side still hits a ceiling.

Sample answer lineAssess the effects of higher government spending on the economy in the extract
Higher spending raises AD, so output rises and unemployment falls.
which economy? use the data

Textbook, not the extract

Data response questions want the figures and the country in front of you. A conclusion about economies in general misses the mark kept for this one.

The method

Know what each mark wants. Before you write.

Every Economics essay is marked for knowledge, analysis and evaluation, and each question size splits them differently. On a 12-mark essay part, 4 of the 12 marks are evaluation, and a one-sided answer gets none of them.

Pick a question

8 marks · Paper 2 essay, part (a)
3K3An2Eval

Explain, then consider. The second evaluation mark is kept for a justified conclusion, so an answer that just stops leaves it on the table.

12 marks · Paper 2 essay, part (b)
8K + An4Eval

An “assess” question. Knowledge and analysis are marked in levels out of 8, evaluation out of 4. A one-sided answer gets no evaluation marks at all, however good the analysis.

20 marks · Paper 4 essay
14K + An6Eval

One essay with no parts to guide you. The top evaluation marks go to a judgement that is developed, reasoned and supported, and that answers the exact question asked.

20 marks · Data response, Section A
2(a)2(b)4(c)6(d)6(e)

A free lesson

One question, four marks, worked

A real 4-mark data response part from May/June 2026: show the effect of a subsidy on production with a diagram, and consider whether it will always work. This is where each mark sits.

  1. The diagram +1Supply shifts right, the price falls and the quantity traded rises, all clearly labelled.
  2. Why it shifts +1The subsidy lowers the cost of production.
  3. What happens next +1Firms supply more at every price, so output and exports can rise.
  4. Will it always work? +1Not if firms keep the subsidy as profit, or if supply is price inelastic and output barely moves.

The drawing earned 1 mark. The other 3 were sentences.

The syllabus

The A Level Economics syllabus (9708), paper by paper

At AS, the Cambridge syllabus covers six areas: basic economic ideas, the price system and the microeconomy, government microeconomic intervention, the macroeconomy, government macroeconomic intervention, and international economic issues. A2 returns to the same themes in more depth. AS students sit Papers 1 and 2. A2 students sit Papers 3 and 4.

Paper 1 · AS LevelMultiple Choice

  • 1hduration
  • 30marks
  • 17%of the A Level

What it asks. 30 multiple choice questions on the AS content, one mark each.

What you practise. Two minutes a question, reading the diagram or data in the question properly, and ruling out the options that are nearly right.

Paper 2 · AS LevelData Response and Essays

  • 2hduration
  • 60marks
  • 33%of the A Level

What it asks. One compulsory data response question worth 20 marks, then two essays worth 20 marks each: one mainly microeconomics and one mainly macroeconomics, each split into an 8-mark and a 12-mark part.

What you practise. Using the extract inside the answer, explaining every diagram, and writing the 12-mark part with both sides and a justified conclusion.

Paper 3 · A2 LevelMultiple Choice

  • 1h 15mduration
  • 30marks
  • 17%of the A Level

What it asks. 30 multiple choice questions on the A Level content, with the AS content assumed.

What you practise. Cost and revenue curves, market structures and calculations at speed, so the harder questions get the extra 15 minutes.

Paper 4 · A2 LevelData Response and Essays

  • 2hduration
  • 60marks
  • 33%of the A Level

What it asks. One compulsory data response question in four parts worth 20 marks, then two 20-mark essays with no parts: one mainly microeconomics and one mainly macroeconomics.

What you practise. Planning an unstructured 20-marker, evaluating throughout the essay, and ending with a judgement that answers the exact question.

How do I get an A* in A Level Economics (9708)?

Knowing the theory is the starting point, not the finish. Examiners reward diagrams that are explained in words, analysis that shows each step of cause and effect, and evaluation that weighs both sides and reaches a justified conclusion. A one-sided essay gets no evaluation marks, so in class you practise the 8, 12 and 20-mark answers until arguing both sides is a habit.

Are A Level Economics past papers enough to prepare?

On their own, no. Past papers and videos show you the questions. They do not mark your answer or tell you which line lost the mark. In the batch, past paper answers are marked and you are told what to change. You can download the A Level Economics past papers free.

What is the difference between AS and A2 Economics?

AS Level is Papers 1 and 2: multiple choice, data response and two-part essays on the AS content. A2 Level is Papers 3 and 4: multiple choice, data response and unstructured 20-mark essays on the A Level content, with AS assumed. We teach the full 9708 syllabus for both.
