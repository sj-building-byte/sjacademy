01 // Biography · Intellectual Arc

The Story Behind the Science

How clinical observations in operating theaters transformed into a decade-long crusade to pioneer mercury-free, tissue-safe solid-state sterilization.

"For decades, healthcare relied on toxic chemicals and fragile mercury vapor lamps that risked human lives. My mission has been demonstrating that narrow-band solid-state photonics can safely sterilize human wounds and clinical airflow without collateral destruction."

My journey began at Sir Syed University of Engineering and Technology (SSUET), where I developed my foundational mastery in electronic circuit design, biosensors, and biomedical instrumentation. Walking through hospital wards, one alarming reality struck me repeatedly: Hospital-Acquired Infections (HAIs). Patients were successfully surviving complicated surgeries only to contract lethal, antibiotic-resistant infections from sanitized surfaces and medical instruments.

Recognizing that chemical wipes leave hazardous residues and conventional low-pressure mercury lamps generate toxic ozone while posing catastrophic glass and mercury disposal hazards, I formed a core thesis: Could narrow-band Ultraviolet-C LEDs at exactly 275 nanometers deliver the lethal photonic dosage required to rupture bacterial DNA and viral RNA, while remaining safe for surrounding human tissue?

To prove this, I moved to Malaysia to conduct doctoral research at Universiti Teknologi Malaysia (UTM) and the IJN-UTM Cardiovascular Engineering Centre. I designed precision radiometric testbeds, tuned surface-mounted (SMD) diode arrays, and conducted rigorous in vitro trials against lethal pathogens including Pseudomonas aeruginosa and Staphylococcus aureus.

The Translational Philosophy: Science That Leaves the Bench

"Real biomedical engineering is never about publishing papers in isolation. It is about closing the loop between mathematical irradiance modeling, bacterial petri dishes, cell viability assays, and actual manufacturable medical devices that doctors and nursing staff can hold in their hands."

Today, my work spans handheld wound-healing sterilization devices, in-duct HVAC airflow sanitizers for pandemic containment, and neural signal acquisition interfaces (BCI). Whether mentoring university students or consulting on medical device development, I stand for rigorous, translational, life-saving innovation.
